**Mike Roeth (VO):** One of the things we look at is day cab production. 45% of the Class 8 tractors that go over the road produced now are day cabs. But also we're seeing that more sleepers are being used in day cab operations.

<!-- timestamp: 00:22 -->

**Mike Roeth:** So if you really study the 10 duty cycles in Run On Less Regional, there's really only one or maybe two that should have a sleeper. But there's really, I think, four that are operating sleepers. So why is that? They may have situations where the driver needs to stay out overnight. So we saw that at C&S, where some of those truckers will actually spend a night on the road, but stay at a hotel.

<!-- timestamp: 00:44 -->

**David Hastings:** On certain days I might have six or seven of them that layover, and it's just one-day layovers. Um, but on a light night it'll be four of them that layover.

**Louis Scaruffi:** It's been good for me. I... I've enjoyed it. I know some drivers, they... they just, you know, frown on having to stay overnight, but that's not a problem with... To me that's part of driving.

<!-- timestamp: 01:05 -->

**Kerry Stritt:** Majority of our drivers are home every night, or at most one night a week they stay out.

**Mike Roeth (VO):** Conversely, some of the fleets operate only sleepers. And maybe that's because most of their trucks at that particular center do multiple nights out, but they have one or two drivers like Dustin with J.B. Hunt where he comes home every night, but they want to have one common truck spec for that location and they use a sleeper for that.

<!-- timestamp: 01:31 -->

**Mike Roeth:** It also gives them some flexibility to use that sleeper in other routes if they need to when Dustin may not be there or they don't have enough of those day cab routes. So predominantly day cabs resonate with regional haul. Day cab is cheaper, they have a lower weight, so good for the duty cycle, but can't serve the whole market. And so we'll probably see day cab versus sleepers in regional haul for some time.
