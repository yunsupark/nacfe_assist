**Jennifer Wheeler:** Warehouse planning for electric vehicles is a lot to think about for a fleet.
Understanding where you're going to put the chargers is a huge factor to accommodate opportunity charging and longer-term consistent charging.

<!-- timestamp: 00:25 -->
Currently, our charging ports are situated next to the breakrooms on the campus to make it easier for our drivers to get to the charging ports and to the trucks. That helps us with efficiency and keeping everything charged as much as possible.

<!-- timestamp: 00:37 -->
**Bill Bliem:** I think there's a location concern of where your chargers are and that you really need to get in upfront discussions with your utility to find out if there is, you know, if you're going to have more demand than they can provide.

<!-- timestamp: 00:53 -->
Potentially, there are new opportunities to drive a vehicle into the warehouse because there's no emission concerns.
There's also less of a need for exhaust fans in a facility.
Fleets also need to consider future proofing as they think about where and how many chargers to add at their facility as they continue to grow their electric fleet.
It's less expensive to tear up your parking lot once instead of every time you add a new vehicle.

<!-- timestamp: 01:20 -->
It is an issue when you're making a million-dollar investment in charging infrastructure into a facility that you don't own the real estate.

<!-- timestamp: 01:27 -->
**Jennifer Wheeler:** Some other things to consider during the planning process are adding solar panels to the facility or battery storage to help offset utility rates and time of use charges.
Fleets should also work with their utility providers very closely throughout the entire process.
This is not a short-term construction process. It can take quite some time.
