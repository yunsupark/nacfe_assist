> Notes: MIKE ROETH, Executive Director, NACFE; Operations Lead, Trucking Efficiency

**Mike Roeth:** Congestion on the roads is a major frustration for drivers. Congestion can be caused by many things: just a lot of cars and trucks in urban areas, accidents on our freeways, construction, and many other things can cause congestion. And fuel economy decreases whenever this occurs. In our first reports from the truck drivers in Run on Less, they're telling us about congestion. One case, we've got troubles with congestion in California, and in the second example, an evacuation from Florida due to Hurricane Irma is already causing slowdowns, congestion, and frustration for the drivers, as well as challenges in maintaining the high fuel economy that we're seeing.

**Clark Reed:** It's really interesting to watch people staying up on people, staying on their tails, kinda close, so people aren't cutting in on you. The problem is, that just makes it stop and go, stop and go - and that just consumes fuel. So I find it best to lay back a bit, maintain a slow steady speed, and if people cut in front of you, you have time to brake for that.
