**Dave Schaller:** It was interesting to me interviewing one of our drivers that his fleet allows him to go back to his home every Friday afternoon. And part of their operational procedure is to make sure that since his home is away from his distribution center that he operates out of during the week, they make sure his vehicle is loaded for his hometown in one of the big box stores in that area, so that every time he heads home, it's not empty miles, it's loaded miles. So he's efficient even on his return trip home on the weekends.

<!-- timestamp: 01:33 -->

**Dave Schaller:** So the opportunities to take technologies in the IT world with big data, with AI, etc., and learn how can I be more efficient in backhaul operations is an exciting area that's developing rapidly and will help our industry moving forward.

<!-- timestamp: 01:51 -->

**Speaker (unclear):** We saw that with these Run on Less fleets. Some of them have a a minimum amount of miles out before they'll look at a backhaul. So if they're two, three, four states away, maybe 500 miles from home, they'll more than likely try to find a backhaul because they've got some time to get that back, deliver it somewhere, get that truck back to the distribution center or home base. Which is filling up these tractors and trailers as much as possible. So backhauls are a very good thing for freight movement as well as fleet profitability as they're gaining more business in that backhaul.
