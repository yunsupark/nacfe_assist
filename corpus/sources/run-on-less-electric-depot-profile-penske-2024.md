<!-- timestamp: 4:20 -->

**John Leroux:** Well, I'll be honest, the first thing I did love about it, it was no vibration in the cab. Uh, it was quiet. The torque was direct. I love the steering on it. Very smooth. It—it doesn't take much to turn it. Beautiful thing I love about this tractor is that I can keep it in an idle, and I'm not—there's no emissions coming from it at all. Uh, which is great. <!-- timestamp: 4:53 -->

**Debbie Shust:** I think it's a revolution. It's a different powertrain, so people want to downplay the fact that it's just a powertrain. But the impact to how we think about what we do every day, I—I really think it's very comparable to when horse and buggy was displaced by internal combustion engine. And I actually have seen a lot of parallels because people have asked me, you know, why should I believe that this is going to happen, and what is it going to be like? But at the turn of the century, there were actually political and social pressures to go to internal combustion engines. And then create the right price point, and in about a 25-year period, horse and buggy was displaced with—with internal combustion engine. And that's kind of what we're saying is going to happen on electric vehicles, that in the next 25 to 30 years, we expect everything to be displaced, and we've got proof points in the past that that kind of transformation can take place.
