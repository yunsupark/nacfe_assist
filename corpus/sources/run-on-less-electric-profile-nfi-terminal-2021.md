**Mike Roeth:** Welcome to Chino, California. Here we're visiting NFI and their sprawling campus of warehouses. We'll be following a Kalmar Ottawa electric terminal tractor moving trailers around the yard and to the docks. Let's go check it out.

<!-- timestamp: 00:35 -->

**Jazmin Vasquez:** My name is Jazmin Vasquez. I'm a yard driver at NFI and have been with the company for two years. I got into truck driving because my dad used to be an owner-operator. So ever since I was little, I would always go with him on the road. And my brother as well became a truck driver himself. So I just decided to say, you know, if he can do it, I can do it, too.

When I first heard I was going to be driving the electric truck, I was actually very excited. I was training in a diesel when I first started here, and then once they brought in the electric trucks, I felt it was very much like a privilege because I know other people don't have that in other warehouses don't have that. What I really do enjoy is I'm able to see everything around me. Noise, feels like it's noise cancellation in there. You don't hear much from the outside. But not just that, I also enjoy the AC. I'm going to mention that. The AC is amazing, especially here in Southern California.

<!-- timestamp: 01:28 -->

**Marco Molina:** On a daily basis, these trucks are covering a campus here in Chino. We have an eight-building campus here. The yards go building to building shuttling trailers in between or in that same yard. In some cases, they do travel maybe a couple miles down the street to shuttle a trailer, and that's their daily activity, and they do this 24/7.

<!-- timestamp: 01:48 -->

**Gina Lopez:** We introduced this generation EV product to the market in 2018. We didn't start delivering our first units until mid-year 2019, and we have about 100, a little over 100 in the field right now operating. We've just been refining this product and collecting a lot of information and data and really making some tweaks to make this product perform on par or better than our traditional diesel product. That was our ambition from the start.

<!-- timestamp: 02:16 -->

**Dean Newton:** They are so much more maneuverable than a day cab truck that you might try to use. It's anywhere from three to 10 times faster to move a trailer with a terminal tractor. We don't go fast like a day cab. We can't take it out on the road and do, you know, 55 mile an hour, but it'll definitely move whatever you want, wherever you want in a short period of time.

**Bill Bliem:** It works. You're in a captive environment. We opportunity charge every chance we get. We've never had a low battery or a dead battery issue.

<!-- timestamp: 02:46 -->

**Marco Molina:** And maintaining the trucks and keeping them charged, that's done, we usually use it intermittently through their breaks and lunches, depending on how the voltage of the truck is. At times, they may be a little lower, so they may need to take an extra break, but it's all done breaks and lunches and in between shifts.

Currently, our charging ports are situated next to the break rooms on the campus. Some are technically in the back of the buildings or in the front of buildings. And we've changed some stuff infrastructure-wise like added doors to gates to make it easier for our drivers to get to the charging ports and to the trucks. That helps with just the efficiency and keeping everything charged as much as possible.

<!-- timestamp: 03:22 -->

**Gina Lopez:** NFI specifically is one of those fleets that really can test their paces. They have some of the most aggressive applications that we've seen of our deployed fleet, driving the longest distances, reaching the higher speeds, the number of picks and drops that they're doing in a given shift, they're on the very high extreme level. So it's allowing us to then refine our product design so that we make sure that we're taking care of those top levels or those top tiers of customer operations.

<!-- timestamp: 03:52 -->

**Dean Newton:** Energy on board could be lessened. They need to do the opportunity charging net anyway. So I would say that would probably be the biggest learn that we've got. The other is power consumed when they're charging the truck is a lot less than I think anybody expected, so their infrastructure side doesn't take as big a hit.

**Jazmin Vasquez:** Hopefully in the future we do see more electric coming in for our environment and for our own health and our future generations to come. I truly enjoy working for NFI as we're making that switch and we make a change.
