**Ken North:** We are in Edmonton, Alberta, Canada, visiting the Kleysen Group, which is part of the larger Mullen Group. Kleysen will be running a Kenworth T880 sleeper tractor with a Cummins X15N engine using natural gas during this Run on Less – Messy Middle as it hauls heavy bulk liquids into a far north region with extreme temperatures. Let's learn about how Kleysen is looking forward to testing the full extent of this natural gas technology.

<!-- timestamp: 00:36 -->
**Phil Rivard:** The site here today that you're looking at is our Edmonton facility and we transload what we call multi commodities.

**Rhonda Watters:** We do a variety of commodities here from our bulk tankers with diesel, gasoline, jet fuel, bio-diesels, along with our flat deck work that we do.

**Phil Rivard:** Our fleet today is roughly 250 tractors. Mullen is very innovative and always researching the newest technologies, the newest alternative fuels. They propose to us that natural gas is probably the most feasible currently in the marketplace today. There are other fuels, but we don't find yet that they have the technologies for class 8 trucks pulling 63,500.

In 2024, we came first to market with the Cummins X15N engine. It opens a lot of doors with additional power, additional capability that wasn't possible with prior natural gas power plants. So when we got our first natural gas truck, we determined that we wanted to operate in some of our harshest environments, which is northern Alberta, from Edmonton, Grand Prairie, to Fort McMurray in particular. We put this truck in the minus 30. It was a lower temperature. And now we're operating in high temperatures, which are hitting 30, 35. And there's no complaint. I don't see nothing wrong. It's operating in the same way.

Right now, we're looking at day trips in particular, so 500 to 600 mile round trips or 1,000 km fully loaded with Super B tankers in particular.

**Ganesh Srinivasan:** What delicately impacts the range and reduces the anxiety for the driver is the fuel tank configuration. That's where the collaboration with the OEMs, the fuel tank providers, etcetera, becomes critical so that you use back the right tank to be able to get the job done.

**Sarah Abernathy:** This truck behind me here is a 244-inch wheelbase and it features a 175 diesel gallon equivalent Cummins clean fuel technology tank at the back of cab. At Kenworth, we offer specific sales codes and spec options for customers to tailor in that wheelbase and get that spec right up to where they need it to be. I'm going to accommodate whatever the fuel or range that they might want to have on that truck.

**Rhonda Watters:** Because of course it is a pilot project, we want to get the right operator in the seat of the truck, somebody that is skilled, knowledgeable, forward-thinking, and that embraces the pioneering of clean energy.

**Marcin Jedrzejczak:** I have over two decades of experience. I did driving in Europe, mostly Germans, France, Netherlands, Belgian, also in England for a couple years, and I'm here over 10 years now. So actually that was my concern about alternative fuel. I thought they won't have the same power as diesel. And I get surprised because it does. When doing incline, it's a little bit faster than diesel. And it's more quiet than diesel. So the ride is it's much better. We don't get so tight because we are tired usually because of the noises from the engines. The truck in the morning it's warming up faster. So I can start working faster. And I really love that. That's a huge advantage in our job. We usually come in the morning, we we get assigned a job to us. The dispatcher will dispatch us or tell what to do. And we start with crew trip, and usually it's a short trip. It's around could be from half an hour to 1 hour. Then we came back and probably we get another job. With highway jobs, we come in the morning, we put up the truck, and then we hit the highway. So we usually go away for for a couple hours. And then could could also could be a long haul jobs, which is uh we stay in the truck for for a couple days.

**Rhonda Watters:** It's not a traditional fuel gauge and it's a really hard thing to wrap your head around because it's not an empty full scenario. So you have to have a bit of uh forethought, okay, I've got this much pressure in the tank. I can go this far.

**Cody Brookwell:** Natural gas being lighter than air. Gaseous fuel, the ambient temperature outside does impact the pressure within the tank. So what we are always talking about and trying to educate the fleet on is the difference between pressure and the actual volume quantity of fuel within the tank. 3600 psi is the industry standard at 20 degrees Celsius. But as you drop down to minus 30 or plus 30, there's going to be a big swing in pressure. But the volume and the range within that truck remains the same. We typically will design the compression of our site to mirror that of diesel.

**Marcin Jedrzejczak:** Refueling natural gas is easy and safe. It's maximum 15 minutes.

**Phil Rivard:** Most of our clientele, they expect us to reduce our carbon footprint, and how can we reduce their carbon footprint?

**Ganesh Srinivasan:** Bringing it back to why fleets look at natural gas as an option, sustainability is a big reason for that. Using RNG, which has a much lower carbon intensity, and in a lot of cases, depending upon the source, even negative carbon intensity, it helps them achieve the CO2 emission reduction goals quicker. And a fleet does not need to go 100% RNG, they could just use a blend. But one of the important factors there, and this brings to the second aspect that fleets look at when adopting it, is the total cost of ownership. So you will have to look at what is the right blend which gives them a balance between the right pricing for the fuel. It's still reduce the CO2 emissions to meet their sustainability goals. Here in Canada right now, access to RNG is not as widely as what we have for CNG. So I think as the infrastructure matures and as fuel becomes more widely adopted, we would see more adoption of RNG in our market out here as well.

**Cody Brookwell:** I always talk about the economics of natural gas because I'm a big believer that, you know, fundamentally it has to make sense with or without funding. And the strength in this technology is in the underlying commodity of natural gas itself. Commodity that is extremely stable and has been for the last decade, and that's just a reflection of the supply and demand balance. We have so much capacity in Canada alone uh to produce in far excess of what the demand is.

**Phil Rivard:** You know, this is our first uh CNG tractor and uh so we've got some testing and proving to do to make sure that this is definitely the route we want to go. We try to stay on the forefront. Obviously, it's sometimes a catching up game, but uh we like to be in the forefront.
