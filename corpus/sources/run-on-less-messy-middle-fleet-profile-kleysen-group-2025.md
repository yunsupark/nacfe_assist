Let's learn about how Kleysen is looking forward to testing the full extent of this natural gas technology.

**Ganesh Srinivasan:** What directly impacts the range and reduces the anxiety for the driver is the fuel tank configuration. That's where the collaboration with the OEMs, the fuel tank providers, etc., becomes critical so that you spec the right tank to be able to get the job done.

<!-- timestamp: 04:24 -->

**Rhonda Watters:** It's not a traditional fuel gauge, and it's a really hard thing to wrap your head around because it's not an empty-full scenario. So you have to have a bit of forethought, okay, I've got this much pressure in the tank, I can go this far.

**Cody Brookwell:** Natural gas, being lighter than air gaseous fuel, the ambient temperature outside does impact the pressure within the tank. So what we are always talking about and trying to educate the fleet on is the difference between pressure and the actual volume quantity of fuel within the tank. 3,600 PSI is the industry standard at 20 degrees Celsius, but as you drop down to minus 30 or plus 30, there's going to be a big swing in pressure.

**Marcin Jedrzejczak:** Refueling natural gas is easy and safe. It's maximum 15 minutes.

**Phil Rivard:** Most of our clientele, they expect us to reduce our carbon footprint, and how can we reduce their carbon footprint?

**Ganesh Srinivasan:** Tying it back to why fleets look at natural gas as an option, sustainability is a big reason for that. Using RNG, which has a much lower carbon intensity, and in a lot of cases depending upon the source, even negative carbon intensity, it helps them achieve the CO2 emission reduction goals quicker. And a fleet does not need to go 100% RNG; they could just use a blend. But one of the important factors there, and this brings to the second aspect that fleets look at when adopting it, is the total cost of ownership.

<!-- timestamp: 05:44 -->

**Ganesh Srinivasan:** So you will have to look at what is the right blend which gives them a balance between the right pricing for the fuel, but still reduce the CO2 emissions to meet their sustainability goals. Here in Canada, right now, access to RNG is not as widely as what we have for CNG, so I think as the infrastructure matures and as fuel becomes more widely adopted, we would see more adoption of RNG in our market out here as well.

<!-- timestamp: 06:29 -->

**Cody Brookwell:** I always talk about the economics of natural gas because I'm a big believer that, you know, fundamentally it has to make sense with or without funding, and the strength in this technology is in the underlying commodity of natural gas itself. A commodity that is extremely stable and has been for the last decade, and that's just a reflection of the supply and demand balance. We have so much capacity in Canada alone to produce in far excess of what the demand is.

**Phil Rivard:** You know, this is our first CNG tractor, and so we've got some testing and proving to do to make sure that this is definitely the route we want to go. We try to stay on the forefront. Obviously, it's sometimes a catching-up game, but we like to be in the forefront.
