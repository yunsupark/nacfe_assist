**Frank Falcone:** Well, this truck really lends itself to a short-haul day use, oftentimes using drayage and delivery applications, with the route as well-known and very repeatable. This is the kind of case where a vehicle like this can offset thousands of dollars per year in fuel costs. What we're looking at here is kind of a country road type of route. So there's some stop-and-go as far as stopping at some stoplights, but there's a lot of cruise and rolling hills. Anytime you're going downhill, of course, you get to recapture some of that energy as the motors brake the vehicle as opposed to the mechanical brakes. And then the vehicle's able to very efficiently climb the hills and ultimately take its cargo to its destination.

> **Slide/Figure — On-Screen Graphic**
> Type: callout box
> Notes: 14Xe ePowertrain

<!-- timestamp: 03:01 -->

**Frank Falcone:** Most people are familiar with the diesel type of powertrain, which is an engine, a transmission, a driveshaft, and a conventional axle. And the prior generation is a motor, transmission, driveshaft, conventional axle. Well, Meritor's innovation is taking that motor and that transmission, building all of that into the axle. And so this new generation truck is using two of these ePowertrains, which frees up all this room in between frame rails. Now we're able to build a truck that has more energy storage on board and a shortened wheelbase, which is more in line with what a typical day cab truck is. This of course improves maneuverability in tight yards and other places that these types of trucks tend to drive.
