# 1 INTRODUCTION

The North American Council for Freight Efficiency (NACFE) was established in 2009 as a 501(c)(3) non-profit to provide unbiased information to all stakeholders interested in improving the efficiency of freight movement. This is accomplished through a combination of creating in-depth reports on technologies for improving efficiency, through industry outreach, workshops and conferences, and through freight efficiency demonstrations like Run on Less.

Run on Less is a biennial demonstration highlighting significant technologies available to the trucking industry. NACFE has conducted five Run on Less demonstrations to date. See Figure 1.

The 2023 Run on Less – Electric DEPOT focused on fleets operating 15 or more BEVs at a single depot as of June 2023.

Today there are multiple powertrain solutions available to fleets including battery electric, hydrogen fuel cells, renewable natural gas, and renewable and bio diesel. NACFE was interested in looking more in-depth at the Class 8 long-haul truck market, which is 9% of the market but contributes 48% of the emissions, according to NREL’s report, *Decarbonizing Medium- & Heavy-Duty On-Road Vehicles: Zero Emission Vehicles Cost Analysis* [1].

The 2025 Run on Less – Messy Middle focused on 13 fleets operating trucks with four powertrain solutions — diesel/renewable diesel, natural gas/renewable natural gas, battery electric and hydrogen fuel cell. The goal of Run on Less – Messy Middle was to bring clarity to the long-haul segment of the market and to learn about the performance of heavy-duty Class 8 trucks — sleepers and day cabs — in long-haul return-to-base and over-the-road duty cycles.

## 2 SCOPE

The purpose of this report is to document the year-long effort to bring Run on Less – Messy Middle to fruition and to provide an analysis of the initial findings and lessons learned from this demonstration.

## 3 NACFE’S MISSION

NACFE’s overriding principle in reporting on technologies is to provide an unbiased perspective. NACFE recognizes that it also has vested interests and an agenda. NACFE’s mission is to improve the efficiency of North American goods movement. NACFE pursues this goal in two ways: By improving the quality of the information flow and by highlighting successful adoption of technologies. NACFE’s Confidence Reports assist in information flow, while the Run on Less demonstrations and Fleet Fuel Studies highlight adoption success and provide benchmarks for fleets. 

NACFE also recognizes that emerging and future technologies need an unbiased review to help fleets consider them and for manufacturers to learn and adapt their products and solutions to better meet fleets’ needs. To that end, NACFE started publishing Guidance Reports on emerging and future technologies. As of mid-2025, NACFE has published 11 Guidance Reports on electric and hydrogen fuel cell trucks. 

NACFE shares its research free of charge to the industry, conducting and participating in information-sharing events including workshops, panel discussions, and conferences.

### 3.1 HOW DID THE IDEA EMERGE?

In the spring of 2024, NACFE staff began discussing ideas for the next Run on Less event, which would take place in 2025. One theme kept recurring: while for some use cases — terminal tractors, vans and step vans, medium-duty box trucks, and return-to-base regional haul — there was a clear pathway to decarbonizing with zero-emissions freight movement. However, things were less clear in the long-haul sector of the industry, and that lack of clarity was causing fleets to shy away from deploying alternative powertrain solutions in their operations. NACFE wanted to evaluate the viability of all the powertrain solutions currently available to fleets to shed light on the benefits and challenges of each solution.

There was debate around whether diesel-powered vehicles should be included in the Run and it was ultimately decided that highly efficient diesel-powered trucks can and should be part of a fleet’s decarbonization strategy. The more miles fleets get from a gallon of diesel fuel, the less fuel they use. The less fuel they use, the less emissions they release into the atmosphere.

Discussion also centered on whether to include hydrogen fuel cell vehicles in the Run given the nascent nature of the technology and the fact that there currently is only one manufacturer of hydrogen fuel cell Class 8 trucks in the North American market. It was ultimately decided that while in their infancy, hydrogen fuel cell powered trucks show promise as an option for long-haul trucking. 

The messy middle solution set may also grow to include even more technologies. A key tenant of the Run on Less series is that it demonstrates commercially available solutions in real-world operations. Some potential future available solutions outside of the scope of this Run are:

# 4 WHAT WAS RUN ON LESS – MESSY MIDDLE?

Run on Less – Messy Middle focused on heavy-duty Class 8 trucks, both sleepers and day cabs, in the long-haul return-to-base and over-the-road duty cycles.

The Run included vehicles with multiple powertrain solutions currently available to fleets, including battery electric, hydrogen fuel cells, renewable natural gas, and renewable and bio diesel.

The goal of the Run was to explain the various alternative powertrain options in multiple long-haul duty cycles.

Thirteen fleets participated in Run on Less – Messy Middle sharing what they are doing to decarbonize their operations. As in previous Run on Less demonstrations, the technology is either commercially available or representative of the vehicle manufacturer’s plans.

While long haul represents a small portion of the total trucking industry, it contributes a significant portion of emissions. If we are going to decarbonize trucking, we need to better understand the options available to fleets operating in that duty cycle and help them determine the powertrain solution that is best for their specific application. Also, given the challenges of long haul and in particular over-the-road irregular routes, this decarbonization will likely take decades, allowing for multiple phases for many fleets. Fleets can decarbonize by dramatically improving their diesel MPG, then use either bio or renewable diesel or natural gas before completing the journey with battery electric or fuel cell electric trucks.

## 4.1 MESSY MIDDLE REALITY

The messy middle is a time between now and when the trucking industry gets to the zero-emission movement of freight [15]. Figure 2 is a graphic representation of the Messy Middle and the various powertrains that will take trucking to a zero-emission function. 

In 2019, NACFE began using the term “messy middle” and it has since caught on and is used by many people involved in the transportation of goods. While the messy middle can be complicated, NACFE does not view messy as bad, but rather sees it as a time where fleets will have to evaluate many options and look beyond just the truck to the infrastructure needed to support that truck. It is critical that fleets have reliable data on which to base their decisions to make proper choices.

# 5 THE PLAN FOR RUN ON LESS – MESSY MIDDLE

The initial goal for the Run was to engage 12 unique fleets in different regions of North America and to feature a mix of powertrain options all in the heavy-duty sector operating in long-haul return-to-base and over-the-road duty cycles. Trucks could either be sleepers or day cabs.

The participating fleets identified their own driver(s), route(s), and loads as part of their normal daily operations. The fleets, manufacturers, and other stakeholders were interviewed on-site at their respective operations during June and July of 2025 prior to the Run for the purposes of hearing from the participants and capturing video for NACFE. The duty cycle, equipment specifications and infrastructure to be used to fuel or charge the trucks, as well as the data collection process was reviewed with each fleet.

Funding for the event was pursued through sponsorships and in-kind contributions. Participating fleets were not compensated outside of whatever publicity they received from taking part in the demonstration and through their own social media efforts and press releases. Nor did the fleets incur costs other than their in-kind support as noted.

NACFE conducted a significant educational outreach effort in the seven months prior to the demonstration through a series of webinars that became the Messy Middle Bootcamp [18].

Social media and the trucking and clean energy media were engaged through a series of fleet profile videos prior to the demonstration. Then videos on a variety of topics related to the Messy Middle were released during the event, capturing comments from the participant site interviews and other interviews with subject matter experts.

The demonstration kicked off with a press release and NACFE introduced the event at major industry conferences throughout the spring and summer. When the Run concluded in September, a press release was issued at a virtual press conference where NACFE shared initial findings with various media outlets.

### 7.1.1 Albert Transportation

Albert Transport started in 1983, and is a small long-haul truckload carrier based in Laredo, Texas [3]. Henry Albert, CEO, believes fleets can do better with diesel-powered trucks than they currently are doing. He is a member of Freightliner’s Team Run Smart and took part in the first Run on Less in 2017. To date, Albert has achieved a lifetime high of 13.7 MPG on his 2022 Cascadia and a lifetime average of 10.5 MPG on that truck.

Albert participated in RoL-Messy Middle with a 2022 72” raised roof sleeper Freightliner Cascadia with a 455 hp Detroit DD15 diesel engine and a Detroit DD12 automated manual .78 overdrive transmission, a 2.61 axle ratio and a 6x2 liftable pusher axle and liftable lead axle on the trailer. The vehicle has the full aerodynamic Cascadia package which includes side skirts, chin air dam, 24” cab extenders pulling a dry van with a nose cone, full length trailer skirting, trailer tail and Flow Below wheel treatments on the truck and trailer. The truck also is equipped with a battery powered dual HVAC system and Merlin solar panels. See Figure 8.

Albert believes that conventional diesel technology still offers meaningful efficiency gains when paired with a driver who treats fuel as a central performance metric.

## 9 SITE VISITS

Visiting each of the fleets prior to the Run is an important and integral part of the overall Run effort. Going out into the field and experiencing the detailed benefits and challenges is crucial to understanding and has been a key strategy of all five Run on Less efforts. NACFE staff along with a professional videographer armed with still cameras, video cameras and drones spend at least a full day at each fleet location. Key management level personnel and the drivers are at the top of the list of interviewees although the fleet does have input into who else will appear on camera.

The day prior to the shoot, the “crew” arrives at the fleet location to scout the site and determine where interviews will take place. Each person being interviewed must sign a release form and a NACFE staff person is tasked with ensuring all release forms are signed.

NACFE has developed a list of “must have” photos that need to be captured the day of the visit and a unique list of questions is developed for each site based on what NACFE sees as the significant theme for the location. ## 11.1 THE SAMPLING RATE EXPLAINED

Understanding the sampling rate of data is important when analyzing any dataset. Geotab’s system uses what they call curve logging, based on the Ramer-Douglas-Peucker algorithm, to reduce the amount of total data while preserving the most important information. This method allows Geotab to reduce the otherwise massive amounts of data collected so the cost and complexity of handling this data is reduced.

For example, if a vehicle is traveling at a constant speed on a straight section of highway, every second of location and speed data are not all that important because they can be predicted using the beginning and ending points of that straight, constant speed interval. How the algorithm is implemented to minimize information loss is one of the proprietary methods Geotab uses to minimize data storage and transfer while meeting the needs of their applications.

The challenge with working with third-party data aggregators like Geotab is that the way the data is collected, simplified, and reported is part of the proprietary knowledge of the company. For typical fleet data users, these details are not significant, and companies like Geotab have made significant investments to assure the accuracy of the data they are reporting. However, engineers and scientists that want to use the data for other purposes such as duty cycle analysis may find this level of data insufficient.

NACFE determined that for RoL-Messy Middle, Geotab’s data was sufficiently accurate for the objectives of the event and subsequent analysis and reporting.

### 11.5.1 Battery State of Charge

Battery state of charge (SOC) is like the diesel fuel gauge in that it is just as problematic and challenging to determine precisely and accurately. Battery SOC for RoL-Messy Middle is a measurement made by the vehicle by continuously monitoring voltage, amperage, and amp hours remaining after fully charging. Basically, this means subtracting amp-hours expended over time from the full charge state. These values can vary based on the algorithm used by the truck OEM and where current and voltage measurements are taken.

Each manufacturer may interpret SOC differently. The batteries have an absolute capacity value at the time they are charged. This value may change over the life of the battery with repeated charging events. The manufacturer also may choose to make portions of the battery inaccessible to the vehicle operator to protect the battery from harsh conditions that might alter the product life.

The battery SOC compared to time passed is shown on the dashboard. One graph is overlayed with speed, and the other is overlayed with distance.

## 12 GAUGING REAL-WORLD RESULTS

Run on Less – Messy Middle was different from previous Run on Less events in that it tracked trucks with different powertrains. The first Run on Less in 2017 featured seven diesel-powered trucks and its goal was to benchmark best-in-class fuel economy. For 17 days NACFE monitored seven vehicles, and the result was that those seven fleets averaged 10.1 MPG over the course of the three weeks of that Run. The real-world result here was that if fleets adopt the right technologies, they can see impressive gains in fuel efficiency.

Run on Less Regional in 2019 tracked 10 fleets operating in regional haul duty cycles. All but one of the trucks was powered by diesel (one truck was powered by CNG). These trucks averaged 8.4 MPG and again a key takeaway was that there are improvements many fleets operating in regional haul can make to achieve these higher MPG levels.

In 2021, NACFE launched Run on Less – Electric to find out how battery electric vehicles were performing in the real world. This time NACFE followed 13 trucks and discovered that for some duty cycles — vans and step vans, medium-duty box trucks, terminal tractors and short regional haul — that there was a strong case for BEVs as a replacement for gas- or diesel-powered trucks. NACFE determined the amount of carbon emissions that would be avoided if all trucks in those duty cycles ran on batteries.

In 2023, NACFE focused on the scaling of battery electric trucks by featuring 10 fleet depots with 15 or more Class 3 to 8 battery electric vehicles operating in the US and Canada. NACFE projected the total power needed if all trucks at all 10 depots were electric.

The goal of Run on Less – Messy Middle was not to compare one powertrain option to another, but rather to show the viability of the four powertrain options — diesel, natural gas, battery electric and hydrogen fuel cell — in the long-haul segment of the trucking industry. The long-haul segment of the trucking market comprises only 9% of the trucks but it generates 48% of the emissions [1]. The point of the Run was to explain the various decarbonization efforts, including alternative fueling options, and to explore the impact of duty cycle on powertrain selection.

The real-world world results are that all four technologies are performing well in this segment of the market and there is no one right choice for all fleets. Rather powertrain choice depends on the intricacies of each fleet’s operation, the length of haul, access to fueling/charging and a host of other factors.

## 16.1 OPERATIONS

The Operations Report will document the operational realities of Class 8 decarbonization across 13 fleets and four powertrains (diesel, CNG/RNG, BEV, and hydrogen). Unlike controlled testing, NACFE analyzed how these trucks performed in active revenue service, focusing on duty cycles, dwell time, and utilization.

Key findings highlight that there is no single "winner." Instead, successful deployment requires matching the specific technology to the right duty cycle, whether that is a predictable regional return-to-base route for BEVs or a high-mileage corridor for diesel and CNG.

The report contains the following sections.

• Introduction: Framing the Messy Middle and the transition from legacy to mixed fleets.
• Methodology: Data collection, validation, energy normalization, and attribution logic.
• Fleet Profiles: Participating fleets and company context.
• Duty-Cycle Archetypes: Operational categories used for comparative analysis.
• Fleet-Level Results: Individual fleet performance across diesel, CNG/RNG, BEV, and hydrogen powertrains, including cross-fleet comparative analysis.
• Cross-Fleet Analytics: Regression insights, terrain sensitivity analysis, and human-factor modeling.
• Future Outlook: Autonomy, AI optimization, and infrastructure development.
• Operational Action Playbook and Conclusions: Practical guidance for fleet decision-makers and key findings

## 16.3 TCO RAMIFICATIONS

This report analyzes real-world data from Run on Less to compare the TCO across diesel, CNG, BEV, and hydrogen powertrains. The report will show that economic parity is not a fixed future date, but a specific operational window defined by duty cycle, infrastructure strategy, and other factors. The findings seek to offer fleet managers a framework for deciding which powertrain belongs on which route over time.

The report will contain the following sections.

• Scope: Including duty cycles, powertrain types and timeframes.
• Report Analysis & Methodology: Including why TCO matters and analysis limitations.
• TCO Components: Including the “controllables,” the “uncontrollables,” California vs. national differences.
• TCO by Powertrain: A calculation of TCO for each powertrain
• TCO by Duty Cycle: Including base-case TCO, sensitivity analysis, and time.
