# Electric Use Cases – Heavy-Duty Regional Haul Tractors

> Notes: HEAVY-DUTY REGIONAL HAUL TRACTORS / THE USE CASE

**Rick Mihelic:** Of all the segments that we talk about converting to battery electric vehicles, the class 8 heavy-duty tractors is probably the most challenging. Now, what makes it so challenging is that there's just so many different uses for class 8 trucks, so many different applications, so many different types of trucks. Now, we found that regional haul is a really good fit for BEVs.

<!-- timestamp: 00:29 -->

**Mike Roeth:** Short and medium regional heavy-duty drive cycles like drayage, shuttles, and food and beverage are a lot more numerous than many people think, and with their return to base operation and short mileage, they are key candidates for electrification. We found that 50% of the regional haul truck market, about 468,000 tractors, are a good case for electric trucks today, where they return to base and they travel generally under about 200 miles.

<!-- timestamp: 00:58 -->

**Bill Bliem:** Electrifying trucks in our fleet just makes sense with our dedicated operations where outbound, our average length of haul is about 300 miles, and we had the perfect fleet we felt to pilot.

<!-- timestamp: 01:13 -->

**Paul Rosa:** The amount of collaboration between component providers with the respective OEMs, I have not seen it any better than it is now. There's so much to learn and that's what this project has been about. Any new technology, we have to have a good understanding of what it can do and what it can't do. So, this is just a normal progression of our learning before we go and educate our field operators, our field sales people, and then thus our customers, that we feel good about the technology, we're ready to make it available for them.

<!-- timestamp: 01:42 -->

**Christopher Cochran:** When I was told a few years back that we were going to test pilot electric trucks here in California, I was ecstatic. And after getting the feedback with the test pilots, working it through with BYD, I have never been so happy to see how far technology has come over the last few years just in logistics alone. It's mind-blowing.

<!-- timestamp: 01:59 -->

**Matt Wetta:** We are working hard to stay abreast of what the latest and greatest technologies are and make sure they're incorporated into our vehicles. I would say in almost every single drive that I've done with a potential customer, almost immediately someone makes a comment about just how silent and quiet the vehicle is to operate. And usually the second comment is about the performance. I think there's a day in the future where all of our transportation is done with zero emissions vehicles. And so this is the first step on the road to that end goal.

<!-- timestamp: 02:35 -->

**Rick Mihelic:** In talking to fleets, we found that the biggest concerns seem to be around the maximum freight you can carry and the maximum range you could go. Now, both those factors are related, but we found that there's a significant segment of regional haul vehicles that cube out, and so they're not carrying the max weight. And then for those vehicles that are operating max weight, there's a significant group that operate in very short ranges, so like beverage delivery for grocery stores.

<!-- timestamp: 03:08 -->

**Gregg Stumbaugh:** This electric truck we run exclusively on one run. It goes from our warehouse in Napa to a bottling facility in Sonoma, and it just shuttles from the bottling facility back to our warehouse in Napa.

<!-- timestamp: 03:21 -->

**Kassidy Kiesel:** We have eight electric trucks here at Inland Empire, and we run them every single day. We try to keep them closer to the branch just to be more efficient with our routes, and we run them as a side loader truck because it's the way we can be most efficient with them and carry the most beer.

<!-- timestamp: 03:34 -->

**Hector Banuelos:** It's about 240 miles round trip. Usually it's one round trip out of one truck. He tries to do two out of the day. So, once he comes in, he'll drop the truck off, hook it up to the charger, and then take the second truck out.

<!-- timestamp: 03:49 -->

**Donald DiSesa:** My day starts at 4:00 in the afternoon. We do deliveries between 8 to 15 different customers. At the end of the day, we end up at a charging port. I pull in, plug it in, I see a green light, and I'm good to go.

<!-- timestamp: 04:05 -->

**Bill Bliem:** We've been working with the OEMs. That was our point when we started this, to help them get their trucks to the point where they can scale. And that's where we're at today is a little more range, a little less weight, and we're good to go.

<!-- timestamp: 04:22 -->

**Rick Mihelic:** The driver shortage is a perennial issue for fleets. Attracting drivers, keeping drivers, those are issues that fleets deal with daily. Now, we found that BEVs are a really good incentive for attracting drivers of all ages and keeping them in the fleet.

<!-- timestamp: 04:39 -->

**Pat Brandon:** I've been driving a total of 35 years, 10 years for Biagi. The first time I ever drove this truck, I was like, "What? You picked me to drive the electric truck?" I could not believe the honor it was to be the first one. And it's amazing because it's quiet. You can hear the radio, you don't have to crank it way up, I hear everything in this.

<!-- timestamp: 05:04 -->

**Donald DiSesa:** I feel honored to be able to drive the electric truck, one of the first guys to drive it on my team. The truck is so quiet, everything is smooth, it gives you time to focus on what's going on around you. With the diesel trucks, there's rattling, there's driver fatigue, things you don't even know are going on. But as soon as I got in the electric truck, I realized this is the way of the future, this is really the way it's going to go.

<!-- timestamp: 05:30 -->

**Rene Solis:** Well, I've been with the company for over 30 years and now I'm driving an electric truck. You know, I always enjoy the physical work. This is what I like, you know, I go home in a happy mood, and the next day I'm the same way. So driving is my passion, I guess. You'll have better understanding around you, you can hear things. So I think for the driver, it's a good thing, because if you hear something, you know something is not right. So I think it's a good and a safe thing to have an electric truck. We have stores, they're curfew stores, you cannot make noise. These trucks, they're perfect, because I can go in the back and I don't bother nobody.

<!-- timestamp: 06:36 -->

**Christopher Cochran:** When people walk up to them outside when they're driving the truck making a delivery, they're like, "Wow, that's the coolest electric truck I've ever seen. Like, how does it work?" And the drivers get all excited, like, "Yeah, you know, if you push this button and it's silent, it's cool!"

<!-- timestamp: 06:49 -->

**Rick Mihelic:** BEVs are exciting, they're capable, and they're helping fleets and their customers get to zero emissions.

<!-- timestamp: 06:58 -->

**William Avis:** Anheuser-Busch loves innovation, so this is an exciting venture because right now we have 21 trucks in Southern California that we're able to look at and be able to reduce CO2 by 462 tons annually with electric trucks. So this is an exciting venture.

<!-- timestamp: 07:16 -->

**Christopher Cochran:** New things are always the scary thing when you try to change a culture, but the buy-in here with the employees is like, "Wow, this is something new and like we actually care about the environment and it's really part of Anheuser-Busch's greener initiatives." So, the employees are seeing that. Make the change, it's well worth it. It's well worth the investment knowing that we're going to set up a better future for, you know, the next generation.

<!-- timestamp: 07:38 -->

**Bill Bliem:** Our company's excited about what's going on. We feel so strongly this is the right thing to do for the environment, we're committed to it. You know, a lot of customers have lofty goals out there of being zero emission, being carbon negative, and we need to help them get there and this is how we're going to do it.
