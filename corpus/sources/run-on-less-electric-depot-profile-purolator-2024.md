<!-- timestamp: 05:15 -->

**Charlotte Argue:** Telematics on an individual truck can help inform that operator what the daily efficiency is of that specific vehicle in that specific circumstance, but where the big power is with telematics data aggregated--so we're talking about big data--is we can anonymize and aggregate hundreds of vehicles or thousands or millions of trips as these vehicles get deployed and use that to analyze really what different factors impact range and efficiency. And we can start to look at things like temperature, and the same thing with speed. As you increase speed, what does that mean overall in terms of loss in efficiency. So the insights can then be pulled back into telematics tools to help that fleet operator understand range prediction and capabilities for their unique situation and the factors that impact them.

**Nader Abumuoilish:** What's unique about the Motiv system is that we have various levels of regenerative braking that the driver in the vehicle can configure himself. So using these up and down arrow keys, the driver can go in and change regenerative settings. So having it on 1 is the least aggressive setting, it'll feel similar to what they're used to, a gas or diesel vehicle. Once they get, you know, a couple weeks under their belt, they can change that to 2 and then eventually get down into 3 where it's the most aggressive. The most aggressive regenerative braking setting is what's ideal in stop-and-go, you know, type of traffic, multiple deliveries in a single block. Now, if you're driving on the highway, you'd probably want to bring it down to the medium setting 2 or even the lowest setting number 1.
