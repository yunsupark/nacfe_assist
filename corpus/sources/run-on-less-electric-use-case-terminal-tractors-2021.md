<!-- timestamp: 00:07 -->
As NACFE explores electrification in the class eight truck industry, one of the best initial use
cases is for electric terminal tractors or yard tractors, hostlers, jockeys.

The terminal tractor market is one of those really growing markets in North America and beyond.
I think one of the triggers for why the growth has accelerated so significantly is associated with
the rise of e-commerce, and sort of the rise of the distribution concept.

The term opportunity charging doesn't mean charging away from home base. It means taking
advantage of breaks in the workers' schedules or charging during lunch periods.

We don't just charge it at one single point. Whenever the driver has an opportunity to do
paperwork, to take a break, he plugs it in. Anytime that the truck is idle and sitting, it
typically is plugged in and charging.

I'm optimistic that the EV is going to save on maintenance costs, increase uptime, and at the end of
the day, it's going to reduce and/or eliminate carbon footprint.

Our strategy when we built this truck was to increase uptime and reduce cost. We saw that
diesel trucks, especially with aftertreatment, had significant downtime, and we wanted to
solve that problem with an electric vehicle.

The electrified tractor doesn't have the aftertreatment technology on it that the diesel does.
Therefore, the maintenance headaches that go with the aftertreatment system are gone.
