**Jessie Lund:** So we get asked a lot, you know, why are these fleets really taking the step forward? Why are they leading the industry here when we know that there are still challenges to be worked out? And I think for a lot of these early adopters, particularly these Run on Less Electric participants, it comes down to three things. A lot of these companies have already committed to significant, ambitious climate and/or sustainability goals, and they realize that it's not just about setting goals, it's about taking action.

**Ian Prince:** Our goal is to become the greenest career company in Canada. You know, we've got a long history of trying new things, deploying new technologies. In 2005, we deployed over 200 hybrid electric vehicles. It's really about continuing that journey, so the next 10 years, we plan to have 1800 electric vehicles on the road.

**Ian Prince:** Recently, in a co-op project with Castrol, we decided to embark on the mission of becoming a carbon-neutral company, and we are the first carbon-neutral race team in the United States to achieve certification. There's nothing we can do to impact our fuel consumption on the track. On the other side, on the support side, we made a big push with Ford Motor Company this year to make our fleet as electric or as hybrid as we possibly could.

**Jessie Lund:** I think also, like any business, it comes down to a bottom line. Total cost of ownership of these electric trucks may not pencil out today, particularly without incentives. There are still financial opportunities for these fleets who are leading the way, whether that's the maintenance savings or lower fueling, in this case electricity costs compared to diesel.

**Julie Ann Lake:** Getting an electric vehicle made sense financially, and it also made really good sense environmentally. If we're installing EV chargers, we have to put our money where our mouth is.

**Jessie Lund:** But also things like driver attraction and retention. We've heard repeatedly that drivers love these vehicles, they're excited about them, and these fleets deploying them and the publicity that comes along with those, really helps fleets attract drivers who are interested in, you know, whether it's having the coolest, greatest technology or just being good community members and utilizing this low carbon, zero emission technology.

**Alex Schaumann:** I feel proud knowing that we've initiated something that, you know, other companies haven't tried yet. I was literally the first person to get trained, I was the first person to test it out on a delivery, and I'm proud to be able to say that.

**Jessie Lund:** I think the third thing we're seeing from these early adopters is really just leadership. Fleets who know that the industry needs to move forward and like any technology that's come a long way recently, it's due to these early adopters really putting the time in, putting the money in, figuring out how to make this be successful as well as how to address the challenges that we all know are out there.

**Bill Bliem:** We feel so strongly this is the right thing to do for the environment, we're committed to it. You know, a lot of customers have lofty goals out there of being zero emissions, being carbon-negative, and we need to help them get there and this is how we're going to do it.

**Susan McSherry:** It helps visibility and for other fleets to see the success of the program and decide, hey, these guys, they're making it work, it's in their best financial interests to do it. It's a game changer.

**Jessie Lund:** We're not going to learn without doing, and so these fleets really in my eyes just should be applauded for leading this industry forward.
