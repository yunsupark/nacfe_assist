**Benjamin Happek:** Hydrogen is from our perspective, great for long haul, for high payload applications where you need a lot of range. Even though we know that we have to overcome certain challenges, especially when it comes to infrastructure and hydrogen availability. We have two fuel cell electric systems on our truck. One you can see here typically where the engine would have been, and then you can see another fuel cell system right under our tank system structure. The fuel cells themselves offer each 85 kW of power. Both fuel cell systems together drive the 350 kW e-motor, together with a 72 kWh battery pack. So think of a fuel cell truck as a hybrid vehicle that has both batteries and fuel cell systems and both power the truck at the same time and when the truck is stationary or decelerating, the e-motor would charge the battery and the fuel cells also have the ability to always keep the battery at a very high state of charge. That is good for the battery durability. We need a battery because especially when we lift off we need a lot of power and fuel cells like to be driven in a more constant power level whereas the battery can really co-power in the beginning and then also offers later when we decelerate or go downhill the ability to recuperate energy and put that back into the battery which helps for driving range and fuel efficiency.

**Benjamin Happek:** So this is our hydrogen tank system. It holds about 70 kg of hydrogen fuel at 700 bar, which is about 10,000 psi. That tells you how high the pressure is if you think about that your car tire may be 35 psi, so these are 10,000. What you can see here is that they are all connected to a common line. So these 10 tanks act as a singular tank, meaning when the truck gets refueled, all of those tanks get filled at the same time at the same rate and offer a total of 70 kg of hydrogen fuel. This tank system has been designed with safety first in mind. So this is a very rigid, strong crash-tested structure, and all of those tanks are equipped with an emergency release valve. If there's a thermal event and the tank would heat up, there would be a very controlled release of hydrogen gas, and all of those tank lines basically go up to the roof of the vehicle and you can see a small cut off at the roof and then the hydrogen would basically vent out there.
