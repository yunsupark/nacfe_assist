**Jessie Lund:** Today the Run On Less Electric team is here at the Port of Long Beach. We're visiting with NFI, who's using Volvo VNR Electric tractors to haul containers between the port and the Inland Empire. Let's go take a look.

<!-- timestamp: 00:34 -->

**Bill Bliem:** Our company's excited about what's going on. We feel so strongly this is the right thing to do for the environment, we're committed to it. You know, a lot of customers have lofty goals out there of being zero emission, being carbon negative, and we need to help them get there and this is how we're going to do it. Electrifying trucks in our fleet just makes sense with our dedicated operations where out and back, our average length of haul is about 300 miles, and we had the perfect fleet, we felt, to pilot.

<!-- timestamp: 01:06 -->

**Keith Brandis:** In 2018, we won an award with California Air Resources Board through South Coast Air Quality Management District called the Volvo LIGHTS project, Low Impact Green Heavy Transport Solutions. The entire ecosystem of freight electrification at key customer sites.

**Brett Pope:** This allowed us to accelerate our speed to market. So here in Southern California, this is one of the first units that we have in operation.

<!-- timestamp: 01:31 -->

**Brett Pope:** The VNR Electric, this is a production unit. This is something that's available now, and what we're doing here is testing and validating some of the energy system storage with our partner NFI in their drayage operations. Very easy to drive, very comfortable, and very quiet. We just created an easy way to transition into a zero emission product with the electric driveline.

**Jeffrey Howard:** My name is Jeffrey Howard. I work for NFI. I've been driving for 3 and a half years.

<!-- timestamp: 02:01 -->

**Jeffrey Howard:** I have a lot of family in the trucking industry. My dad is, my grandpa is. It's something I enjoy doing now. We do two runs to the port usually every single day if we have time. It's about 240 miles round trip.

**Hector Banuelos:** Usually it's one round trip out of one truck, and tries to do two out of the day. So once he comes in, he'll drop the truck off, hook it up to the charger, and then take the second truck down.

**Jeffrey Howard:** It's a good truck. It's very strong, it's quiet, it's smooth. It's like you're not holding anything. It's very strong.

<!-- timestamp: 02:31 -->

**Bill Bliem:** We've been working with the OEMs, that was our point when we started this, to help them get their trucks to the point where they can scale. And that's where we're at today is a little more range, a little less weight, and we're good to go.

**Brett Pope:** The VNR Electric has our short bumper to back of cab, so that allows for that tight operation in city environments. Our energy storage system is lithium-ion batteries.

<!-- timestamp: 02:59 -->

**Brett Pope:** The propulsion system for the VNR Electric consists of two electric motors with a two-speed transmission. These electric motors provide 340 kilowatts of power or 455 horsepower. And we have a two-speed transmission because this still is a tractor that's got to pull 82,000 pounds. So we have that lower first gear for launch capability, for acceleration, then we shift into second gear and we have the wide operating band of the electric motors to provide a high-efficiency driveline.

<!-- timestamp: 03:36 -->

**Keith Brandis:** This is a way of not only piloting, but also proving and improving upon the next generation of EV vehicles. I would say five years ago, I didn't believe electric trucks were a possibility, much less had a road for success. So I've been proven wrong. The technology is transformative.

**Bill Bliem:** We know we can make it work here, and we can work out the bugs that we need to work out to get it into those other dedicated operations and move it across the country.

<!-- timestamp: 04:07 -->

**Keith Brandis:** When I'm on college campuses trying to recruit young engineers, young IT specialists, business majors, it's fun now to talk about something that's not seen as the typical smoke stack manufacturing industry, but to bring up what we're doing to improve the air quality, to make trucks safer, and especially the way that vehicles are connected and speaking to one another. It's a great time to be in the transportation business.
