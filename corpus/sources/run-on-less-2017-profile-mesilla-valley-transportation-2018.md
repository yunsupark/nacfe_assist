<!-- timestamp: 00:49 -->
> **Slide/Figure — ALLAN DAHRINGER / Maintenance Director, Mesilla Valley Transportation**
> Type: lower-third graphic
> Notes: ALLAN DAHRINGER; Maintenance Director, Mesilla Valley Transportation

**Allan Dahringer:** New aerodynamic mirrors. We have a hood mirror that is also very very aerodynamic looking. This is an eight battery bank. We are running all-ACs as far as hotel load, start, and APU. All charged by solar panel which is mounted to the roof cap. Full fairing package on this LT model. Fuel tank is covered. We're fully skirted all the way to the back here. 6x2 axle configuration, six wheel positions, two being the steer, two being the driven axle, and two being what I call the tag axle or the dummy axle. We have a very narrow gap between the fairing and the trailer. I believe we're down to 23, 23 1/2 inches. In the event you have to make a U-turn, we have a stripe on the trailer that's just prevention of damaging that that cab extender right there. We have a stripe right here, a vertical stripe which we put on every trailer. So if you do make a 90-degree turn, you can still see that stripe in your mirror. If you go anywhere past that or if you lose sight of that stripe, that's when a decision needs to be made. Do I continue with this U-turn or do I stop and seek a different route?

**Allan Dahringer:** Wheel covers, of course. We've got this truck so streamlined, all the air just passes by. Serrated wind pass through mud flaps, does have an effect on fuel economy. And we have installed a Wabash skirt, we call it a multi-section skirt. It also has air release. We have an opening right here and this also covers the landing gear. Most skirt packages only come up to the landing gear, so we're sealing that. We have our trailer tails, very fuel efficient.
