**Chris Trajkovski:** Our focus is certainly fuel economy, but it's also balancing that with reliability and uptime. We've worked closely with not only the manufacturer, but also their technical experts to make sure that we get the engine parameters dialed in.

**Ken Marko:** We've implemented downspeed engines and transmission combinations with lower rear axle ratios. And we've also implemented automated manual transmissions to help with driver efficiency as well.

<!-- timestamp: 00:57 -->

**Mike Roeth:** So if they know that a truck is going to be in the mountains or on the flat, or hauling heavy or hauling light, hot climates, cooler climates, all those factors can be designed into the truck via different engine parameter settings across the entire powertrain. There's a lot of variety in the engines. We have 11-liter, 13-liter, 15-liter engines in this run.

**Dan Scherer:** We've dropped down from 100-gallon tanks down to 80 and 50. We're running a 2.41 rear axle. It's a 6x4 configuration because we operate here in Michigan.

<!-- timestamp: 01:28 -->

**Mike Roeth:** And we also have 4x2s, 6x4s, 6x2 liftables, and that all depends on the freight being hauled, the routes being run, as to which one of those technologies are most valuable for the fleet. And it's not all about saving fuel. This is about total cost of ownership.

<!-- timestamp: 01:46 -->

**Jim Boyd:** So one of the shifts we've seen in the last couple of years for us has been a shift to 4x2 tractors. In the earlier days, we ran 6x4 tandems, then we transitioned to 6x2. Our current model has us moving more and more to pup operations, twin 28-foot trailers, and a 4x2 just offers so much greater fuel economy than a 6x4 or a 6x2 tractor can offer.
