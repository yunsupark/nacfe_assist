> Notes: BRIAN JOHNSTON, VP, Program Management and Regulatory Compliance, Lightning eMotors

**Brian Johnston:** So, what you see behind you is an electrified version of the Ford Transit T350. What we do under the hood is we electrify all the components that would normally be found on your fan belt. Instead of an alternator, there's a 12-volt DC-to-DC converter, there's a electric power steering pump, and an electrified air conditioning compressor. We also need to electrify a source of heat, since you no longer have a hot engine.

<!-- timestamp: 02:13 -->
> **Brian Johnston:** This vehicle has a 43-kilowatt-hour battery, which is located under the back of the vehicle. That provides it with a dynamometer-tested range of 61 miles. One of the advantages of the Lightning compact design and unique battery placement is that the cargo compartment of the vehicle is not sacrificed in any way.

<!-- timestamp: 02:30 -->
> **Gordon Culver:** The bread trucks are bigger, they're bulkier. They're not great for delivering in places like uptown and downtown where we see more residential deliveries. And the vans, you can deliver everywhere. They're big enough that you can do commercial stops in them, they're big enough that you can have some of that area that's right around our building.

<!-- timestamp: 02:43 -->
> **Ben Mandel:** Each new generation of truck that's rolled out, they have a procurement cycle, and they're willing to kind of keep pace with that technological evolution.

<!-- timestamp: 02:50 -->
> **Gordon Culver:** This past year, we put in over a dozen new chargers. We knew where we wanted them, we knew how to install them. And honestly, whenever new electric vehicles arrive at our facility, we'll be more than ready to be able to handle them.

<!-- timestamp: 03:01 -->
> **Brian Johnston:** This deployment has been very important to Lightning eMotors because this was a pilot program with nine vehicles. They've been operating it since November, and they've already followed on with a large additional order. There'll be 55 more of these coming to Manhattan, and another 30 going to the state of California.

<!-- timestamp: 03:16 -->
> **Susan McSherry:** For DOT, trying to electrify the last mile is really where the agency and the city would like to see freight move towards. And so we really do welcome these kinds of demonstrations and really are happy that they're coming to the Northeast and being demoed in the Northeast because weatherwise, there's not always going to be the environmental sweet spot for an EV here. Very cold winters, very hot summers.

<!-- timestamp: 03:41 -->
> **Gordon Culver:** Our drivers have so much to deal with every single day on the road that could cause their day to go the wrong direction. And I think the trucks that we have now, they can get in it and know that at least the vehicle won't be a problem.
