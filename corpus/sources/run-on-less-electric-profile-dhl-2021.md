**Mike Roeth:** Here we are in the Big Apple of New York City. This is where DHL brings packages in from all over the world, and they distribute them across Manhattan to residential and commercial customers. They do that with a group of electric vans built by Lightning eMotors.

<!-- timestamp: 00:40 -->

**Brian Johnston:** So, what you see behind you is an electrified version of the Ford Transit T350. What we do under the hood is we electrify all the components that would normally be found on your fan belt. Instead of an alternator, there's a 12-volt DC-to-DC converter. There's an electric power steering pump and an electrified air conditioning compressor. We also need to electrify a source of heat since you no longer have a hot engine.

<!-- timestamp: 01:07 -->

**Gordon Culver:** For a long time, our couriers would actively go grab a gas vehicle if they could because they knew exactly what they were going to get. That's not the case anymore. You know, a lot of them gravitate right towards the new trucks that we have, knowing that it's the best truck we have, and it's going to drive the way they want it to, and they're going to have the nicest drive while they're out there working.

**Antonio Grimila:** I actually like driving the smaller vehicles more because, you know, you can get around a lot easier in Manhattan. Help out the environment and cut down on the pollution, and still get the job done just the same. Well, the drivers who use them every day love them.

<!-- timestamp: 01:35 -->

**Susan McSherry:** It helps visibility and for other fleets to see the success of the program and decide, hey, these guys, they're making it work, it's in their best financial interest to do it. It's a game changer.

**Ben Mandel:** DHL is a great example of kind of a global steward, a lot of really interesting early adopter activity in Europe, and now in leading North American cities as well. So that provides more data for groups like ours to help to validate and understand how those deployments are performing in the field.

<!-- timestamp: 02:04 -->

**Julie Johnson:** The medium-duty space, this is the category where we can really optimize battery capacity and performance along with the operational needs of an urban setting.

**Brian Johnston:** This vehicle has a 43 kilowatt-hour battery, which is located under the back of the vehicle. That provides it with a dynamometer tested range of 61 miles. One of the advantages of the Lightning compact design and unique battery placement is that the cargo compartment of the vehicle is not sacrificed in any way.

<!-- timestamp: 02:30 -->

**Gordon Culver:** The step trucks are bigger, they're bulkier, they're not great for delivering in places like uptown and downtown where we see more residential deliveries. And the van you can deliver everywhere. They're big enough that you can do commercial stops in them, they're big enough that you can have some of that area that's right around our building.

**Ben Mandel:** Each new generation of truck that's rolled out, they have a procurement cycle, and they're willing to kind of keep pace with that technological evolution.

**Gordon Culver:** This past year, we put in over a dozen new chargers. We knew where we wanted them, we knew how to install them, and honestly, whenever new electric vehicles arrive at our facility, we'll be more than ready to be able to handle them.

<!-- timestamp: 03:01 -->

**Brian Johnston:** This deployment has been very important to Lightning eMotors because this was a pilot program with nine vehicles. They've been operating it since November, and they've already followed on with a large additional order. There'll be 55 more of these coming to Manhattan and another 30 going to the state of California.

**Susan McSherry:** For DOT, trying to electrify the last mile is really where the agency and the city would like to see freight move towards. And so we really do welcome these kinds of demonstrations, and really are happy that they're coming to the Northeast and being demoed in the Northeast because weather-wise, there's not always going to be the environmental sweet spot for an EV here, very cold winters, very hot summers.

<!-- timestamp: 03:38 -->

**Gordon Culver:** Our drivers have so much to deal with every single day on the road that could cause their day to go the wrong direction, and I think the trucks we have now, they can get in it and know that at least the vehicle won't be a problem.

**Antonio Grimila:** [laughs]
