**Speaker (unclear):** Welcome to the NACFE Technology Day on connectivity. The central question we are trying to answer today is, can connectivity improve trucking efficiency? We all are seeing the impacts of being connected. Today, we are connected to everyone and everything via that little device that most of you are holding in your hands right now. This is maybe the biggest innovation in history, but also comes of course with many challenges, including authenticity, privacy, and security.

<!-- timestamp: 00:46 -->

**Mike Roeth:** What we're seeing in trucking, and why connectivity is a technology day and a webinar, is that it offers a lot of opportunity for us. So whether we're connecting a truck to another truck, whether we're connecting the truck to the infrastructure, or the truck to the fleet with streaming data, all that powerful data that's now connected, we can understand it and learn it and make decisions based on it, is huge. I hear all the time that, "Now the data's proven me wrong. I thought I knew this about my trucks, I thought I knew this about my drivers, and now I'm finding it's very different than what I thought."

<!-- timestamp: 01:20 -->

**Jeff Baer:** There's always some surprises. What we like to do is let the data speak. And because I've been around trucks and trucking my whole life, I have a lot of preconceived notions about what the data's going to say, and my team has taught me, and our data has taught me, that I don't always get them right. And so, I'm sure there are going to be some surprises. I think along the order of maintenance issues, you know, we'll probably find one or two of these trucks along the line might have had an issue with one of the technologies that might not have been evident in real time.

<!-- timestamp: 01:49 -->

**Magnus Koeck:** Connectivity for us is key. Connectivity is actually the basis for a lot of things we do, not the least uptime, but of course efficiency. And you know, we monitor all the trucks, and we can monitor exactly how they run. Are they doing too much on idling? Why is that fleet more efficient than the other? What's the spec? And we help our customers out, so we can do so much more with the data.

<!-- timestamp: 02:12 -->

**Jeff Baer:** So to me, I can't help but see the autonomous vehicles out there decades away, likely, at scale, that would use our data or data like ours to operate as efficiently as the best drivers in North America.

<!-- timestamp: 02:26 -->

**Mike Roeth:** On this panel, we have a fleet, PepsiCo, who's looking at how to take advantage of this data. One of the things they're doing is being able to understand the weight on the truck real time, and figuring out, should we put more beverage on, take more off? We also have Cummins, who's helping to look at each individual engine, maybe looking at it from afar, so they're looking, checking in with the truck, looking at the engine, maybe even customizing the engine over the air, which allows it to be more efficient in the particular route or duty cycle it's taking on. 

<!-- timestamp: 02:57 -->

**Mike Roeth:** We also have on the panel, Joyce Tam from Peloton, looking at not only connectivity from one truck to the other to allow closer following distances and much safer operation, to also a situation where that information's shared with the cloud, and make decisions on whether we should platoon or having a following truck in different weather conditions, that sort of thing. With Noodle.ai, they're looking at decisions that can be made to prevent downtime. So, pulling data off of the trucks, like maybe a high water temperature, where they know that that typically means a water pump's going to fail in the next 100 miles or 200 miles, and being able to understand that, get that truck in the shop, schedule its repair, and get it on the road. Maybe we can do those repairs and avoid a downtime, increasing productivity and efficiency.
