**John Richardson:** Hi everyone, today we're in Long Beach, California and we are at the WattEV site at the port of Long Beach. So today we're going to take a look at the Nikola electric trucks that they have here. We're going to take a look at the charging infrastructure and we're going to talk about charging-as-a-service and we're going to talk about trucking-as-a-service. WattEV has developed their own charging hardware here. They've also future proofed the site to support megawatt charging because they believe the future of trucking is megawatt charging.

<!-- timestamp: 01:11 -->
**Salim Youssefzadeh:** So WattEV was created to accelerate the transition to electric heavy-duty trucking. And when we first started the company several years ago, we wanted to be the main infrastructure provider. So we started off with the idea of building out truck charging infrastructure in strategically marked locations, with our first site starting in Bakersfield, and other in San Bernardino, Gardena, and the one in Long Beach, which we're at today. We started with the idea of "build it and they will come" but very quickly realize that we can't just build and have, you know, the demand not generate naturally. So that's when we started looking at electric truck as a service concept and how we can sort of prime the market and really accelerate the transition by bringing both the infrastructure and the trucks to help both the shippers and the carriers meet their sustainability goals. So there's a lot of barriers that are stopping both shippers and carriers from getting into this. Shippers may not want to go and build infrastructure behind the fence. They may not have the capital to do so, they may not have the land to do so, and they may not have the power to do so. And then on the carriers, they also are faced with the same issue of how do I purchase a truck that's three to four times as much? Where am I going to charge? How often do I need to charge? What happens on the other end? And that's why we came up with, you know, the truck as a service concept where we ourselves have been testing the routes and then going to the carriers and saying, "Hey, we've tested this, we know that we can take an electric truck from the port to an inland empire with charging on both sides." So we're providing the electric truck, we're providing the charging, we're providing the maintenance and the damage insurance, all at a fixed price per month.

<!-- timestamp: 02:42 -->
**Chinmay Jaju:** As WattEV actually started, you know, breaking ground on some of the sites and getting trucks delivered, we started getting, you know, more and more serious with them about, hey, well, when when can we actually get one of these things and drive it onto our network? I think some of the magic that happens with a company like WattEV working with a company like Uber Freight is we're really good at supply demand matching and we have an incredible amount of scale. So when we think about a company like WattEV who is always going to have, you know, really incredible opportunities they're finding with, you know, target shippers, in almost all cases there's always going to be some amount of holes in a schedule where the truck is underutilized. You can get pretty darn efficient with like a really good customer that has high density, but there's always going to be gaps and that's really where Uber Freight can come in and help.

<!-- timestamp: 03:30 -->
**Bruce Kurtt:** The shippers are the ones that are going to push this. The shipper is looking for their scope 1, 2, or 3 emissions. And a shipper in lieu of building a new assembly plant or a new manufacturing plant, they can have their trucking company show up with zero tailpipe emissions, which allows them to get at their scope 3 goals. So it's a quick way for a shipper to hit their ESG goals.

Underground there's at least four conduits that are going to each dispenser, than another three that go from the the power cabinet to the dispenser itself. So that's a tremendous amount of conduit work and work that goes into the civil and and electrical portion of this.
