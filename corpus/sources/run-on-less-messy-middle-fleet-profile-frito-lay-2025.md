<!-- timestamp: 03:06 -->
**Adam Buttgenbach:** When we first launched this program and this pilot with Optimus, there was definitely some skepticism around biodiesel. Going back a decade ago, there were definitely some challenges even at the B20 level. There's a couple of unique things about the Optimus system that really makes this system very elegant in the way that it's put together. The system isolates B100 to a single tank, so you still have another tank that does have just regular diesel or renewable diesel in it. And so the truck will start and stop on regular diesel, but then when it's cruising down the highway, doing the majority of its operation, it's able to run on that up to 100% biodiesel and have a significant reduction in our emissions. 

And then we've also looked at the improvements in the quality of fuel. The Clean Fuel Alliance of America, as well as some of the national labs like NREL, have done studies on the improvement in the quality of fuel, including improvements in the ASTM spec. And some of those improvements have given us the confidence that we have the ability to be able to operate on biogenic fuels at a much higher level than historically we were able to.

<!-- timestamp: 04:04 -->
**Scott Offerman:** So over here we have our B100 tank that has the heat exchanger in it. Once we get to about 90 degrees Fahrenheit, our Vector Manifold is what I call the brains of the operation. The Vector Manifold is where we pull that fuel out of the tank, make sure it's up to that 90 degrees Fahrenheit. The ECU, or Electronic Control Unit, is right here. Once it recognizes its condition and it's ready to go, we'll send it upstream to under the hood, where it'll go to our fuel selector valves. And there's two valves typically on our configuration, and those valves are going to select whether it's running on diesel fuel or if it's running on B100. So once it's conditioned, it's ready to go, we'll close off the fuel supply of ultra-low sulfur diesel and open it up so that we can hopefully do 85% to 90% of the duty cycle on B100.

**Adam Buttgenbach:** We're excited to launch the new Volvo VNL into our fleet. Really, the new VNL has really focused on a lot of improvements around efficiency and safety. **Duane Tegels:** The cab itself is a little bit narrower in the front versus tapering out to the back side of it. The fuel economy we've seen is run anywhere between 6 and 10 depending on what the customer's duty cycle is and the type of aerodynamic features that they've selected on this truck. So when we look at the new D13 engine, we've done some internal improvements to it. We've changed the engine geometry just a little bit. And then as we transition back to the axle, the axle is a—this is a 6x2 tractor, so it does have a liftable axle, reducing the parasitic loss. The 217 also has a nice benefit as it allows the truck to spend more time in Eco-Roll. **Randy Long:** Truck Wings is a smart aerodynamic product that's designed to deploy at highway speeds. That deployment closes the gap in between the truck and the trailer. That gap is a major cause for drag. When we seal that gap, what we do is we increase fuel economy, we reduce emissions, and it's all with no driver interaction at all. It's based on the speed. So when the tractor gets to 52 miles per hour, the telematics that are included in the system deploy. When the tractor gets to 51 miles per hour, they pull back in. They get out of the way for tighter turns so they don't damage anything. 

So one of the things that you see about this particular set of wings is we've actually cut these windows into it. That was important because this truck actually has windows in the back, so that allows the driver to be able to see through the wings as they're in a closed position like this. One of the key features of Truck Wings is we actually have the top. So many side fairings will close the gap in between the truck and the trailer. One of the benefits of Truck Wings is it actually closes the gap at the top as well, and that's where a lot of that turbulence happens. And that's one of the major benefits of Truck Wings.
