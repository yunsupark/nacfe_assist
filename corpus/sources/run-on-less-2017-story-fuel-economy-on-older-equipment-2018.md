**Mike Roeth:** So in our work over the last few years, I've had the opportunity to
talk to a lot of drivers and also a lot of owner operators, many of which are very
serious about their fuel economy. And in this story from the road, we're going to go
beyond the seven truckers that are out there in Run on Less, and focus on a couple
of truckers that are really passionate about fuel economy and freight efficiency,
both driving older equipment. That is Steve Crone and Alec Caseras, both driving for
Landstar.

<!-- timestamp: 00:33 -->
**Mike Roeth:** Alec talked about aerodynamics. He has a nose cone on the front of the
trailer and this is very important as he deals with head winds, cross winds, tail
winds. He has really worked on how to tuck the tractor and trailer very close
together and make that gap almost disappear. He has a tail, but he also talked a lot
about maybe the most aggressive side skirt on his trailers, manufactured by Windyne
Fairing Company, very close to the ground, and it covers the entire length between the
landing gear and the rear tandem. That skirt has a flip-and-slide set of features where
it'll telescope as you move the rear trailer tandem for weight changes, and it'll
also flip to be able to have access to the underside of the trailer, which allows for
a very low skirt-to-ground clearance whenever you have a disruption or or someplace
that might damage the skirt, it just flips up.

<!-- timestamp: 01:23 -->
**Mike Roeth:** So when I asked Alec, how do you get to these high levels of
performance? He was very quick to answer that question, that it starts with
information. Really key for Alec is to understand the weight that's in the trailer,
using technologies to look at the air suspensions and predict the understand and and
calculate the weight in the trailer, but also good feedback on his driving habits,
how he's driving, what the shifting is doing, how the truck is performing. Lastly,
he has a lift axle on his trailer. So with that, he can actually lift that axle and
remove that rolling resistance from the road to again increase fuel economy. Alec
shared that driver coaching tools were very important to increasing his fuel economy
over the last year or so and he blatantly admitted that, you know, he didn't think
he needed the help, you know, he's already in double digit fuel economy and it
really did help Alec. So I think the lesson here is that we can always get better
and tools to help us get better can really matter.

<!-- timestamp: 02:17 -->
**Mike Roeth:** And finally Alec offered this sage advice: he said "Work with others.
Be open about your fuel economy and find out what the industry is doing," whether
it's other drivers, fleets, manufacturers, the press and media. He particularly
wanted to thank actually a couple of the drivers in our run: Henry Albert and Joel
Marrow of Ploger. Both had given Alec advice over time and Alec is now sharing his
advice with others. He believes that's one key thing to improving and high fuel
economy of these tractor trailers.

**Mike Roeth:** Steve is well known for getting around 10 miles per gallon on a
15-year-old International truck. He talks about how he improved the fuel economy of the
front of his truck after hitting a deer. Can he actually use the 2017 model-year Mack
front end? Steve's passionate about all aerodynamics, invented his own tail or
Backdraft it's called. This helps the fuel economy on the back of the truck. He loves
driving in the rain. I say, "Well, why?" Because when he stops, he's able to see how
the rain has caused dust and mud to cake and and drive around the around the truck,
and he can see areas of high disturbance or high turbulence. Those are areas that
then he'll go work on, whether it's the trailer or the tractor, to lower the drag and
improve the performance of the tractor-trailer. Steve also hates anything on the truck
that doesn't help. So anything in that airstream, whether it's the license plate,
whether it's lights, grab handles, anything like that, um Steve removes to really
clean up the truck and get great fuel economy from this old truck.
