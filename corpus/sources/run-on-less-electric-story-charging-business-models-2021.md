**Jessie Lund:** The first is to basically run it through a normal procurement process for the fleet to spec out what they need, um purchase it, and have it installed themselves, as well as manage the charging throughout the day.

**Jimi Netniss:** We've been with Frito from the beginning. **Simon Horton:** In order to become familiar with the trucking industry, we've done a lot of outreach with the fleets that operate in our areas. **Jessie Lund:** That said, some fleets may not wish to take on all of that learning, they may not have the internal capacity in order to, um really optimize charging and they might want to rely on an outside expert, a third party who has a lot of experience planning and managing charging infrastructure.

And those fleets might be interested in what's known as a charging as a service model.

Here, paying a third party with expertise in charging infrastructure to help plan and manage your charging, ensure that they're delivering the electrons you need to operate your vehicles for your duty cycles.

<!-- timestamp: 01:56 -->
> **Slide/Figure — Charging Business Models: Charging as a Service**
> Type: title slide
> Notes: CHARGING BUSINESS MODELS / CHARGING AS A SERVICE / - Pre-agreed price / - Third party liaises with utility

**Jessie Lund:** And typically, fleets will agree on a certain price, whether it's a price per kilowatt hour or maybe a price per mile of range delivered from those electrons, that they pay that third party. And the third party then would work with your utility to help ensure that everything is set up appropriately.

**Simon Lonsdale:** And it's really the idea of of bundling all of those different, uh aspects of electrifying the infrastructure into that single price per mile, so that the the customer doesn't have to pay separately for design. They don't have to pay for deployment. They can roll that amortize that cost into their uh their operational costs. They're priced for mile or priced for kilowatt hour, uh as well as bundling in, uh if the customer chooses the energy and then energy rebate.

**Jessie Lund:** And a third business model, kind of the next step in this progression would be what's known as electrification as a service.

> **Slide/Figure — Charging Business Models: Electrification as a Service**

**Jessie Lund:** And that's an option where fleets might consider using a third party not just to purchase and manage their charging infrastructure, but also to purchase and manage the vehicles themselves.

And that way, you know, fleets who maybe are a little bit more risk averse, don't want to take on some of the challenges around managing the battery on the vehicle, uh maintenance, ensuring uptime. They're able to have this third party manage that for them, and they can focus on, you know, delivering the goods, um working with the drivers that they hire and just focusing on ensuring that customer satisfaction.

**Jed Proctor:** One of the key things that we've learned is that it's something we as a manufacturer really need to we need to learn about, we need to own and operate and control, uh the experience our customers have with charging. So we're bringing to market our Detroit eFill charging solution, uh where we can own that interoperability, and when there is a problem, our customers only have one phone call to make.

**Steve Clevett:** We think also that this is going to mirror the ESPC business in a lot of ways, where clients are going to look for a guaranteed economic result related to TCO.

**Jessie Lund:** So regardless of your needs as a fleet, there is a business model available for you to help you as you pursue your electrification goals.
