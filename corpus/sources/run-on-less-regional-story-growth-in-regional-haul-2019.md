**Rick Mihelic:** It's becoming more and more of a regional world, especially with e-commerce, more warehousing. The length of the trips are getting shorter.

**Mike Roeth:** The same things that are causing the length of haul to drop, things like e-commerce, technology on the trucks around routing, being able to pick up freight, load matching, we're seeing all that change in how long a truck is out there before it does a drop or does a stop.

Another aspect of regional is relaying of freight. You see that with a couple of the Run on Less fleets.

**Kerry Stritt:** We try to get our road drivers 450 to 500 miles a night, and being in a next day carrier, we have to do things rapidly. And we'll have drivers meet at a midpoint and swap loads. That way drivers can get back to their home base and they don't have to stay out on the road overnight.

**Darin Salgado:** I go to California every day into desert center and meet another driver from California. And we do the same thing there, swap trailers. You check and make sure that the equipment they brought you is safe to run and bring that back.

**Shane Sattler:** Ploger has seen an increase in regional haul. Uh, with this younger generation that's coming up now, they want to be home more. You know, they want to spend more time with their families. It's not always about the efficiency, it's about making the driver happy. The drivers are the ones that run the company.

**Dave Schaller:** We see regional haul growing for a variety of reasons. One of the key reasons is that the variety of alternative fuels that are available to the truck industry is on a continual increase.

**Mike Roeth:** So as we look at more natural gas trucks, and and even more importantly maybe electric trucks, where the infrastructure needs to be built out for fueling or charging, if we know where these routes are, if they're dedicated predictable routes, where we're more confident where we can put that charging and that fueling, could be even hydrogen.

And so with that, there's more confidence that if we put that infrastructure in place, it's going to get used because those trucks are on that route already. Regional is a prime opportunity for these alternate fuels that are coming.
