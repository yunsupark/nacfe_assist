Survey results were tabulated then analyzed by NACFE staff. Once the data was analyzed, several findings were identified.

Fleet and Industry Member respondents both identified working with utilities as the biggest hurdle to electrification. This result is consistent with NACFE’s findings from Run on Less - Electric DEPOT. This demonstration found that installation of power delivery and infrastructure takes too long, and that site planning, utility approvals, and permitting all contribute to delays in electrifying depots. Electrada, the sponsor of this survey, provides additional insights into the challenges fleet face when working with utilities. In a September 2024 blog post, the company pointed out several complexities fleets may face on their electrification journey in working with utilities. These include:

* Understanding a fleet’s energy requirements (short-term and long-term)
* Upgrading utility infrastructure
* Managing the interconnection process
* Negotiating rates and contracts

Managing ongoing charging infrastructure operations is one of the main reasons for working with a CaaS Provider

Respondents were asked in which areas partnering with a CaaS or other service provider is beneficial to a fleet. This also was formatted as a multi-answer question, allowing survey takers to select all responses they felt were appropriate. Generally, Fleet and Industry Member respondents agreed on the top three responses, although in slightly different magnitudes. Fleet respondents indicated that an outside service provider is most beneficial in the planning of a fleet electrification project while Industry Member respondents believed a fleet benefits most by having an outside provider manage the ongoing operation of the charging infrastructure (Figure 6).

Again, this is supported by findings from NACFE’s work on BEV implementations. In video interviews NACFE conducted for the Run on Less - Electric DEPOT participant profiles, several interviewees made the point that outside service providers such as a CaaS provider can help fleets avoid the burden of managing the charging infrastructure and provide access to the diverse expertise needed in the planning and execution phase of the projects. NACFE’s report Electric Trucks Have Arrived: The Use Case for Heavy-Duty Regional Haul Tractors cites a presentation given at NTEA’s Work Truck Week 2022 where several key points were made related to the importance of maintaining the charging infrastructure, including the fact that sometimes fleets had not planned for the long-term maintenance of their chargers, and that charger downtime can result in truck downtime.

Operational Effectiveness is most important when determining whether an electrification project is considered a success

In order to determine how success is measured in a fleet electrification project, the survey asked participants to rate a number of factors on a scale ranging from Not Important to Very Important in terms of how they evaluate whether an electrification project is considered a success. These were then assigned a value of one to five in order to calculate an average response for each of the factors. The average values for each are shown in Figure 7.

Fleet respondents viewed Operational Effectiveness to be the most important determinant in whether an electrification project is successful, followed by Total Cost of Ownership, Customer Satisfaction and Emission Reduction. Industry Member respondents generally agreed with Fleets as to the top three factors but ranked Emissions Reduction as less important than some other considerations, such as Maintenance Simplicity and Driver Experience. Respondents from both Fleet and Industry Member groups agreed that among the listed factors, Public Perception was considered least important when judging the success of an electrification project.

Other Findings
Of the Fleets that have already implemented or are currently implementing an electrification project and that provided a response to the question regarding which outside services they hired, 33% of respondents say they did not use an outside provider while 38% reported using a CaaS or a managed charging provider. In the follow-up question on why no outside provider was utilized, 50% said they wanted to learn for themselves while 37% believed it was not cost effective (this was a multi-answer question and some respondents provided more than one response). Of those same respondents who said they completed an electrification project with no outside service providers, 25% said they would use a CaaS or other service provider in the next project.

Finally, Fleets were asked whether internal or customer goals require reporting of emissions. 46% said internal goals require it, 37% said both internal and customer goals require it, while 18% said neither.

Conclusion

The findings from this survey are consistent with findings in other studies, including NACFE’s Run on Less – Electric and Run on Less – Electric DEPOT. Electrification requires an understanding of an entirely different ecosystem consisting of utilities and charging equipment of which respondents may only have had passing familiarity previously. Working within this new ecosystem is therefore a barrier to fleet electrification, which has resulted in new service providers that help address obstacles such as managed charging and CaaS.

Fleets face significant challenges in developing charging infrastructure and in the ongoing operation of the infrastructure, if pursued on a do-it-yourself basis. Combined with the responses indicating that operational effectiveness is most important in a fleet’s definition of success, it stands to reason that fleets turn to experienced outside service providers to help drive their fleet electrification programs. Nearly 40% of the respondents in this survey did just that, using either a CaaS provider or managed charging service. Of the ones that did not use an outside service provider, more than half cited cost as a prohibiting factor and just over a third said they wanted to learn for themselves (which itself likely includes understanding the cost dimensions so fleets understand the financial ramifications of developing and managing the charging infrastructure). Given that only some fleets operate their own pumps for legacy fuels such as gasoline and diesel, as electrification projects scale up over time, they may find that they do not want to operate charging stations either.

This survey provides an interesting glimpse into fleet electrification challenges now. Respondents to this survey are likely more experienced on this topic than the industry as a whole, as evidenced by the large number of respondents who indicated that they have implemented multiple projects and consider themselves knowledgeable. However, given the level of electrification within the industry today, even the most experienced fleet is only in the very early stages of their electrification journey.
