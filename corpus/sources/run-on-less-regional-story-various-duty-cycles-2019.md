**Mike Roeth:** So we have 10 trucks in Run on Less. It's fascinating to meet personally that they're so different. Different freight they're hauling, different routes they're going. We've kind of characterized those in three areas. One is an A-B-A route.

<!-- timestamp: 00:21 -->
**Darin Salgado:** I go to California every day, and uh meet another driver from California. Swap trailers. You check make sure that equipment they brought you is safe to run and um bring that back.

**Mike Roeth:** So in that duty cycle, very little city driving, very little congestion he deals with, gets on the freeway and goes.

A second is what we call hub and spoke, where it goes out and back, but it might go to a different place on Tuesday versus Monday and throughout the week.

<!-- timestamp: 00:46 -->
**Dustin Whitener:** I'll go to San Antonio, Houston, Austin, Shreveport, Oklahoma City, Lake Charles, Louisiana. Just depending on what you got, you may have three or four or six stops.

**Mike Roeth:** And then finally we have what we're calling an A-B-C-D-A route. And that's a route where generally diminishing loads where it'll go out to one, maybe grocery store, drop some freight, go to another one, maybe drop a trailer, pick up another trailer, go to another store, another store before returning to base.

But as we found, there's duty cycles within each of those three.

<!-- timestamp: 01:17 -->
**Tommy Cottingham:** If you make a truck too specific for a particular region or particular customer, we lose flexibility. So if we spec a truck with the assumption that it's going to run its entire life on flat ground, uh something happens at the account, you know, whatever, they downsize or something like that, or we need to move assets and those assets get moved say into the Blue Ridge Mountains to Tennessee, all of a sudden you've got a truck that's not going to perform well.

**Mike Roeth:** The key for the industry is to design products and to make decisions based on how these technologies will work best in each of these duty cycles, while knowing that we can't design a truck for everyone.
