**Mike Roeth:** And then finally we have what we're calling an A-B-C-D-A route. And that's a route where generally diminishing loads, where it'll go out to one maybe grocery store, drop some freight, go to another one, maybe drop a trailer, pick up another trailer, go to another store, another store before returning to base. But as we've found, there's duty cycles within each of those three.

> **Slide/Figure — Lower Third**
> Type: on-screen text
> Notes: TOMMY COTTINGHAM
> Director, Maintenance Equipment Procurement, J.B. Hunt

**Tommy Cottingham:** If you make a truck too specific for a particular region or a particular customer, we lose flexibility. So if we spec a truck with the assumption that it's going to run its entire life on flat ground, something happens at the account, you know, whatever, they downsize or something like that, or we need to move assets, and those assets get moved, say, into the Blue Ridge Mountains of Tennessee, all of a sudden you've got a truck that's not going to perform well.

**Mike Roeth:** The key for the industry is to design products and to make decisions based on how these technologies will work best in each of these duty cycles, while knowing that we can't design a truck for everyone.
