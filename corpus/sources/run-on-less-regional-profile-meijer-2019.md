**Rita Bare:** About 20 years ago, I needed to make a career change. So I looked into truck driving and I liked the open road. I grew up on a farm, so I've been around big, heavy equipment all of my life, and I find that being a truck driver fits my personality. We haul grocery and general merchandise. I start at 4:00 AM with about 15 other drivers.

<!-- timestamp: 01:03 -->

**Rita Bare:** And a typical day at Meijer, we make between 10 and 12 stops. Most of them are drop and hooks. Then we have some live loads, live unloads. At Meijer, we do what's called a pedal, where you could have product for three or four stores on one trailer, and so you go from one store to the next and make those deliveries. We also do backhauls. So after we drop and hook a trailer at a store, we may go to a vendor location and pick up something like ice cream and take that back to the distribution center. I start my day and end my day at the distribution center, so I'm there every day, and sometimes three or four times a day. While I'm taking my 10-hour break, the fleet technicians, they will be working on my truck. Next morning when I come in, my tractor is ready to roll. I get the same tractor every day.

<!-- timestamp: 01:55 -->

**Dan Scherer:** Meijer has been SmartWay certified since 2009, but this is the first year where it's official now, and this truck is a SmartWay certified truck. On a day cab, it's not usual to run aerodynamic skirts, but we are. We're also running the aerodynamic hubcaps, aerodynamic mud flaps. We've dropped down from 100-gallon tanks down to 80 and 50. We're running a 2.41 rear axle. It's a 6x4 configuration because we operate here in Michigan—Michigan winters.

<!-- timestamp: 02:23 -->

**Dan Scherer:** We're running the DD13 Detroit Diesel engine with the DT12 automated manual transmission. We have progressive shifting programmed into that transmission. We're running low rolling resistance tires, disc brakes both on the truck and trailer, air tire pressure systems on the axles. This is a posilift axle, so when you're running less than 18,000 pounds, the axle will automatically raise, and saves you on wear and tear on your tires and brakes.

<!-- timestamp: 02:56 -->

**Dan Scherer:** On the trailer, running the diesel-electric hybrid. It's a Tier 4 rated diesel engine, but when we're plugged into a dock, we're running on electric. That meets the local noise ordinances for our stores, and also for the emissions. And it's substantially cheaper to run on electric than it is diesel. We're running ice cream spec on our reefers, that's four inches of insulation both in the walls, ceiling, and door. This is kind of unique to us. This is what we call a mini placard, and it's got eight different flip charts on it. Our drivers were initially just writing down what was on the trailer, but it's not something you can see readily, tack it to the gladhand lock. And so we developed this to make it simpler for them to go ahead and give you an indication from off in the distance as to what the status is of this trailer.

<!-- timestamp: 03:48 -->

**Dan Scherer:** One other thing that this reefer unit has is it's got a solar panel, and it doesn't seem like a whole lot, but, you know, when a trailer sits somewhere for a long time, the battery can run down. When it runs down, then it has to start the diesel engine to recharge the battery. Well, the solar panel can recharge the battery for us, so that also eliminates a lot of needless wear and tear and fuel costs on the engine.

<!-- timestamp: 04:15 -->

**Rita Bare:** Well, I would say, hands down, the best thing about regional haul driving is being home every night. I get to be home with my family, I get to see friends more often, I get to go to school events and social functions much more than I used to. Well, the thing that I really like is I can foster relationships. When I go out to the stores or vendor locations, if you go to those places a lot, you learn people's names and a little bit of something about them, and that can only help you. It will never hurt you. I think the most important thing that I would like the general public to know is that having a skilled trade is equally as important and equally valuable as a college education. We have an aging workforce. We need younger people to start filling in, learning and using skilled trades.

<!-- timestamp: 05:05 -->

**Rita Bare:** Truck driving is a skilled trade. It takes patience, skill, dedication—all the same attributes that one uses in a professional trade.
