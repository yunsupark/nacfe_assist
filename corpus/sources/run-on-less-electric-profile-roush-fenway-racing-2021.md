# Electric Profiles – Roush Fenway Racing

<!-- source: YouTube video, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hPMW6UXES9A -->

<!-- timestamp: 00:00 -->

**Dave Schaller:** Welcome to Charlotte, North Carolina, the home of some very loud and fast stock cars, and a legitimately fast vehicle that is very quiet.

<!-- timestamp: 00:25 -->

**Ian Prince:** Our goal has always been to try to maximize the triple bottom line: people, place, and profit. We want to make sure that what we're doing benefits the company in the long run, but also benefits all of our employees and benefits the environment. Recently, in a cooperative project with Castrol, we decided to embark on the mission of becoming a carbon neutral company. And we are the first carbon neutral race team in the United States to achieve certification.

<!-- timestamp: 00:53 -->

**Ian Prince:** One of our major goals was trying to, obviously, reduce our fuel consumption. There's nothing we can do to impact our fuel consumption on the tracks. On the other side, on the support side, we made a big push with Ford Motor Company this year to make our fleet as electric or as hybrid as we possibly could.

<!-- timestamp: 01:12 -->

**Chelsea Jenkins:** Electric right now is gaining a lot of momentum. It's moving out of the light-duty space and into the medium and heavy-duty space. And we're working collaboratively with a lot of stakeholders to understand what the challenges are with deploying this technology in a heavier application, building on the experience we've gained over the last 10 years with our medium-duty gaseous fuels program.

<!-- timestamp: 01:34 -->

**Ian Prince:** The CleanTech truck, we use that for parts and distribution within our campus and then locally here as well.

**Michael Johnson:** The majority of our trips are made to local, nearby race teams that we help support, or they're helping to support us, usually within a 35-mile radius. The strangest comment I've had, I was actually driving down I-85, I had an individual pull up alongside and rolled his window down, gave me a thumbs up, said, "I don't hear a thing." And I said, "Great, you're not supposed to." It's a wonderful vehicle, and I thoroughly enjoy driving.

<!-- timestamp: 02:11 -->

**Jefferson Reed:** Our Generation 1 product is based on a Ford F-650 chassis. When we get the truck, it has a gasoline engine and transmission. We take that out and convert it to our electric system. Under the hood, we have our power distribution unit, our DC-to-DC converter that takes the high voltage and converts it into low voltage for the low-voltage system that runs the cab of the truck, our charger, and our cooling system for the inside, the cab, the HVAC system, and then the cooling system for the drive motor. Over here, we have the charge port. We use the J1772 charge port and our high-voltage on/off switch.

<!-- timestamp: 02:45 -->

**Jefferson Reed:** Back here between the frame rails, we have our two batteries, 400 volts, 138 kilowatt-hours. They're located between the frame rails for increased safety and a better center of gravity. They drive the Dana electric motor that goes to the stock Ford rear end. The ROUSH CleanTech battery electric vehicle is equipped with a 12-volt liftgate.

<!-- timestamp: 03:07 -->

**Michael Johnson:** It's interesting to talk to people when you get stopped. It's an F-650, that's a pretty large truck. And they're amazed that we're able to put that type of vehicle with 1,400 foot-pounds of torque and over 300 horsepower in relation to a gas engine, and have a 14,000-pound payload.

<!-- timestamp: 03:27 -->

**Ian Prince:** The fleet of electric vehicles that we have are primarily charged here on the campus. We also have public charging stations available for the general public to come and use with their charge accounts as well. And the rest of the time when the fleet is not here, they're being charged at home.

<!-- timestamp: 03:41 -->

**Chelsea Jenkins:** We're extra excited about using the data that we gain from the Run on Less Electric demonstration, and taking all of that information and feedback back as we continue to develop Generation 2 of our electric technology.

**Michael Johnson:** I heard someone say it yesterday, we are at the flip phone stage of electric vehicles. Wait till tomorrow when we have the Android and the iPhone, and let's see where that takes us. It's going to be exciting.
