**Steve Jarosinski:** Well, spec'ing for efficiency really starts with the chassis. Here we have a Cascadia by Freightliner. It's a P4, next gen Cascadia you might hear it called. Under the hood, we have a DD15, 400 horsepower, 1850 foot-pounds of torque, coupled to a DT12 12-speed automated manual transmission, and then a low-ratio rear end. Full-length cab extenders, and then basically trying to minimize that gap between the tractor and trailer, and then also even tucking the trailer up as far as we can while still meeting weight requirements. Trying to smooth out the truck here: aerodynamic wheel covers with our aerodynamic inner drive axle panels, front and rear, and then finally onto the trailer for the side skirts.

<!-- timestamp: 02:23 -->

**Steve Jarosinski:** Low front bumper, lower ground clearance here helps us control the airflow underneath the truck, which is very important for fuel economy. Obviously, MPG isn't just about driving. It's the total fuel you consume. So idle is a big part of that, and to get away from some idle, we will use either diesel APUs or battery-powered HVAC systems. Regional is becoming more and more popular, so we do have specialized specs that kind of balance that need of city versus strictly highway to make them as efficient as possible. Regional haul has its fair share of challenges that over-the-road may not, but safety, on-time delivery, and then getting the driver their promised time at home are things that we do very well.

<!-- timestamp: 03:03 -->

**Steve Jarosinski:** The truck driver's job isn't easy. They are driving a very big piece of equipment. It moves slowly, it takes room to maneuver. So I would ask the public's patience: give them room, don't cut in front of them, don't ride their blind spots, things like that. Safety is paramount. Be patient.

<!-- timestamp: 03:19 -->

**Michael Tam:** The most powerful difference between regional and long haul is you're home every week. You know, even if you have a just a day off or two, when you leave that house on the Monday morning, you know you're going to be home Friday, and it's beautiful.
