**Narrator:** Key findings include:

> **Slide/Figure — Findings Overview Graphic**
> Type: graphic infographic
> Notes:
> FINDINGS
> - Small depots are ready for electrification now and electrification at large depots is gaining momentum.
> - There have been big improvements in trucks and chargers since Run on Less - Electric in 2021.
> - The industry needs cost and weight reductions to improve the total cost of ownership.
> - It's still taking too long for power to be delivered and infrastructure to be installed which is driving the use of portable/temporary charging.
> - Range can be extended with multiple charges per shift at the depot and en route.
> - The diversity, passion and capability of the people involved is helping to scale electric trucks.

**Narrator:** There have been big improvements in the trucks and the chargers since Run On Less Electric in 2021.

> **Slide/Figure — Key Finding: Small Energy Depots Are Ready Now**
> Type: graphic infographic
> Notes:
> SMALL ENERGY DEPOTS ARE READY NOW
> Small energy depots are ones that require less than 10 MWh per day due to the small quantity of trucks and/or low miles traveled per day. This could be small urban depots or those that have very few trucks.
> YEAH! DON'T WAIT - DEPLOY NOW!
> For 100% EVs at the site: Up to 10 MWh/day
> LESS COST AND TIME TO ENERGIZE THE DEPOT
> LOW TCO: Electric Vans and Step Vans have up-front costs much closer to parity with gas and diesel
> Many depots, especially those in urban settings, only allow daytime deliveries, causing trucks to sit overnight at the depot, allowing long, slow, more inexpensive charging.
> Slower speed chargers using managed charging significantly lowers costs.

**Narrator:** Small energy depots are ready now, and large energy depots are gaining momentum.

> **Slide/Figure — Key Finding: Large Energy Depots Are Gaining Momentum**
> Type: graphic infographic
> Notes:
> LARGE ENERGY DEPOTS ARE GAINING MOMENTUM
> Large energy depots are ones that require a high amount of power - exceeding 35 MWh per day - due to a large number of total trucks operating that are going long distances, sometimes being driven during two shifts.
> HOLDING OFF ON EVs? TIME TO RE-EVALUATE YOUR PLANS.
> Range is increasing: 250 to 1000 miles per day (WOW!)
> Pepsi Sacramento depot demonstrated ranges of 940 miles on a single day, NOT charging en route and 4076 miles in 24 hours.
> Vehicle Efficiency: 1.7 to 2.9 kWh/mi (CONSIDER RANGE IN MILES PER DAY, NOT MILES PER CHARGE)
> Fast charging and overnight charging: 360 kW to 1 MW
> Slip Seating & Opportunity Charging: Return-to-base or en-route charging (ZAP!)
> Total Site Power: 35+ MWh (Schneider S. El Monte depot projected at 52 MWh per day)

**Narrator:** But energizing the site takes too long.

> **Slide/Figure — Key Finding: Energizing The Sites Takes Too Long**
> Type: graphic infographic
> Notes:
> ENERGIZING THE SITES TAKES TOO LONG (AARGH!)
> For the 10 depots in the Run it took... 9 to 36 months to energize the infrastructure.
> TEMPORARY/PORTABLE CHARGERS CAN HELP: Trucks are arriving before charging is in place, sometimes months earlier.
> Due diligence is needed to ensure reliability of power and to avoid brownouts.
> LARGE POWER DEMAND: Small depots require less, but power is a significant issue for medium or large energy depots.
> ROADBLOCKS TO ENERGIZING: Site planning, utility approvals, site permitting, etc. all contributing to too much time to energize the depots.
> UTILITIES MUST SHORTEN ENERGIZING TIMELINES
> TACTICS TO MITIGATE INCLUDE:
> - Use a consultant with experience
> - Add an early phase to get started
> - But, to get 100% complete electrification quickly and possibly in a single effort

**Narrator:** Another interesting finding is that the diversity, passion, and capability of the people involved in this transformation is helping to scale electric trucks.
