**Tim O'Neill:** PG&E has a number of programs that we're running for the state of California that are funded by the California Public Utilities Commission. EV Fleet is the second program. It's designed to assist customers with installing their charging infrastructure when they're putting into service medium or heavy-duty electric vehicles. We just assist the customers financially and design-wise with trying to plan out and get their infrastructure in, so when their vehicles show up, they actually have charging stations and can operate their vehicles. When you have a customer who wants to get a large charging station for these types of trucks, it does take much more planning, and we usually have to do much more of an upgrade onto our system.

<!-- timestamp: 04:39 -->
**Abraham Leanos:** Once we started this, we did some load calculations, and we were able to just find the power in our facility to be able to get this project of Phase 1 completed.

<!-- timestamp: 04:49 -->
**Jonathan Yan:** It's amazing. It's two years in the making. At the time, this was just completely full of trailers, I think, but now to see the trucks and the chargers here brings a smile to my face. For this area right here, it's about 600 kilowatts. You have three 180-kilowatt power cabinets powering three dispensers each, for a total of nine dispensers.

<!-- timestamp: 05:09 -->
**Job van Campen:** We make real industrial products. As you can see, it's very straightforward. No display, it's functional, needs to perform all the time. So that's our main focus, and that's how we can set ourselves apart: high uptimes, reliability, and very intensive uses. The power conversion unit, it goes to three dispensers, where we can dynamically route power in steps of 60 kW. So if the first vehicle arrives, it gets full power, 180 kW. If a second vehicle arrives, it gets 120 and then 60. So that's maximum usage of the available power to utilize the power unit the best way possible, really.

<!-- timestamp: 05:45 -->
**Abraham Leanos:** We had the power ran over here, and we had a lot of leftover power since we moved out all the truck shop operations to our other facility here. We were able then to allocate the power and use it for the Orange EV chargers.

<!-- timestamp: 05:58 -->
**Zack Ruderman:** Yard trucks really are the optimal truck for electrification for so many reasons. Whether it's the start-stop that helps with regen braking, the inefficiency of the diesel versions, the challenges with uptime for the diesel versions of yard trucks, the fact that the charger can be right there on site, so there's no issues with range anxiety, and when it's designed right, you just don't need that much power, so you don't need huge battery packs to be successful. So we've designed this truck so that it can do two-shift operations, three-shift operations. We have plenty of operations that are 24/7.

<!-- timestamp: 06:33 -->
**Johnny Saenz:** So typically, we pick up majority of our trailers in our transportation yard. We back them up into a door, they get loaded, and then within a couple of hours, we pull them out, we stage them for a route. The drivers, they love it. They love that the fact that they're quiet, it's a clean vehicle. They love the fact that it's such a smooth ride compared to the diesels that we had prior. Everybody wants to jump into it when they get to work, so that's a good thing. As for the charging, it's extremely easy. We just literally plug it in, pop the switch, and charging. And then when we're done, we push the switch back in, unplug it, and that's it. Typically, we never—through a whole day, I would say we never get it lower than 40% per day.

> **Slide/Figure — EV Fleet Process Steps**
> Type: text list overlay on video
> Notes: 
> eVfleet
> DESKTOP REVIEW
> SITE WALK
> PRELIMINARY DESIGN
> CONTRACT ISSUED
> 5 YEAR PLAN

**Tim O'Neill:** Phase 2 is actually the first phase that they got a hold of us with EV Fleet. We were able to complete our desktop review, which helps us determine how much power we have available from the substation where they want to set up their charging stations, do the transformer tap, how big of a transformer we have, that sort of thing. Then we do a site walk. We have an engineering firm that works for EV Fleet, they came out and did the site walk, walked everything down, confirmed where they want the charging stations. After the site walk, they put the preliminary design together, they submit that back to PG&E, and we review it. And once it meets cost thresholds and design criteria and everything that we need, we can go ahead and issue a contract based upon what the customer's told us how many vehicles they want each year over a five-year period. Because we don't want to come back and re-trench after, you know, two years and they have to re-trench again. So we try to help the customer out, we do their five-year plan, so once they put this infrastructure in, they're good for five years.
