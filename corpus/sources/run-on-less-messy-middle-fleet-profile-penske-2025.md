> Notes: Infrared Communication / For truck to communicate with dispenser

**Patrick Brittain:** So what we have on the front side of this nozzle is IR comms. So that's infrared communication. And that's how the truck is communicating with the dispenser. And so it's giving a feedback loop of what the temperature of the gas is going into the truck, how full the truck is, and what the temperature of the tanks are. So we're constantly monitoring the temperature, and if it ever starts to creep too high out of that temperature range, then it'll abort the fill and shut everything down until the tanks have cooled back down. So how we facilitate that coldness is obviously the chiller up top there, and we'll get the gas down to temperatures around -30 degrees Celsius.

**Benjamin Happek:** In the US, we have right now 56 trucks on the road. 35 are being deployed in two projects that have been funded by the government in California: 30 in Oakland and 5 in Southern California. And then we have 21 trucks in our own captive logistics operation at HMGMA, that is Hyundai Motor Group Metaplant America in Savannah, Georgia, a newly built facility in Georgia where we are building all of our future electric vehicles, up to 500,000 per year. What we decided is we want to have a holistic zero-emission approach and want to feed the plant exclusively with fuel cell electric zero-emission trucks to make it a very compelling package. Hydrogen offers a fast refueling speed. It offers more payload for actually hauling freight around. And of course, you have the range. Our truck is in Switzerland showing a range of 250 miles because that's what the customers need and we are fulfilling that. And here in the United States, our vehicle has up to 500 miles of range available for our customers, which is still maybe not as good as a diesel truck who may go as far as 1,000 miles, but it's way better than what most of the battery electric vehicles are currently offering.
