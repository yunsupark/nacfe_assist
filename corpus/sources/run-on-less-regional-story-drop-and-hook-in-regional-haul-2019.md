**Mike Roeth:** Drop and hook is almost like a slang term in trucking for leaving one trailer and picking up another one. Maybe it's a... it's freight that's going to be unloaded in a couple hours, but you as a driver don't need to sit around and wait for that, so you can drop that, pick up another, and be on your way. Think about doubles, so maybe you drop one trailer and you run the front trailer somewhere else, drop it, maybe pick up two more. So that whole drop and hook is

**Mike Roeth:** very valuable, actually, from an efficiency standpoint, because it allows the truck to keep moving, removes idle. There are some challenges with dropping and hooking. Can you imagine being told as a driver to go get trailer 673 and it's over in spot A5, and you take your truck over there and there's nothing there in A5, and you're like, "Oh my gosh, now where is that trailer?" And you got to call back or drive over. Trailer tracking is helping, where we're having telematics devices on trailers so we more confidently know where these trailers are.

<!-- timestamp: 01:06 -->
**Mike Roeth:** Hirschbach Trucking in Tennessee, he literally does six drop and hooks in one driving cycle.

**Mark Casey:** A typical day, I'll do three runs from Monterey, Tennessee to Lebanon. So I'll haul three loaded to Lebanon, three empties back.

<!-- timestamp: 01:20 -->
> **Slide/Figure — Speaker Identification**
> Type: lower-third text
> Notes: MARK CASEY, Driver, Hirschbach

**Mark Casey:** That averages six trailers a day, 30 trailers a week, all drop and hook. I can either get delays in my day through the loading delays or I can get additional delays with the reefers not being cold enough before I'm allowed to leave the property. Being prepared or showing up a little bit earlier to make sure your trailer's hooked or it's preset at the right temperatures are all little things that will add up to help you get on your way efficiently. Nothing worse than to be loaded, ready to go, and your trailer's 10 or 15 degrees hotter than it needs to be.

<!-- timestamp: 01:55 -->
**Mike Roeth:** As we have shorter hauls and more dedicated routes, drop and hook is going to be probably increasing. And so anything that can help us drop and hook more efficiently... maybe there's some automation in that whole process that can be done: automatic fifth wheel engagement, automatic landing gear. You know, some of those things will be helpful.
