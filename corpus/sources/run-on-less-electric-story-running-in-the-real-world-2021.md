Trucks operate in the real world.
The real world has weather, has traffic, it has mountains, deserts and cities.
It has delays, it has hours of service rules, inspections, permits and violations.
The real world has downtime for these trucks, things break and get repaired.
It has costs, profits and losses, and real competition.
The real world has real drivers, real payloads.
The real world is really tough on trucks.
These stories from the road is our daily insight into this real world.
Over the course of the next 18 days, we will provide you visibility into the reality of the current operation of battery electric trucks, their hopes, excitement, challenges and lessons learned from these early adopting fleets.
One of the things I highlight is is the amount of collaboration that is happening in the industry.
Y'all have to work for the common good.
To have battery electric vehicles or zero emission vehicles as soon as possible.
Is it harder than we thought?
Yes.
We didn't realize that infrastructure was going to take more and be more challenging than the trucks themselves.
We found that out pretty quick.
We are at the flip phone stage of electric vehicles.
Wait till tomorrow when we have the Android and the iPhone and let's see where that takes us.
It's going to be exciting.
I think it's an awesome thing to be able to do something for our environment, for our planet, and for our future, honestly.
We're looking long-term here and being able to have a game plan to make things sustainable, and to be part of that is truly awesome.
The greatest thing is the opportunities of what can we do with this technology beyond what we already have.
Where can we take this next?
