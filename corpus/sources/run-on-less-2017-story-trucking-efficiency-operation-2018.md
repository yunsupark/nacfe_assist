North American tractor-trailer fleets spend $105 billion per year on fuel. Average tractor-trailer in the United States, sleeper kind of tractor-trailer will do 125,000 miles a year and at current fuel costs and current efficiency, that's around $70,000 in fuel per truck per year. The fleets can save $40 billion per year on fuel. Putting that hard-earned money back in the hands of fleet owners and operators. There's opportunity to save 30, 40, 50% on their fuel costs with a lot of these technologies and a lot of practices that they could adopt. There's technologies available right now. Why aren't fleets moving those into the marketplace? How do we get good information out to knock down the barriers and the holdbacks that are keeping fleets from taking up new technology that's available today that has payback today that can help them today?

The aspiration is to double the fuel efficiency of the North American fleet. The operations interested in increasing the scale and the adoption of products that already exist in the marketplace. For a tractor-trailer, we've identified 70 different technologies that are available to fleets to do that. Trucking efficiencies is going to help generate demand for these technologies. We're looking to fill the gap in credible, third-party, unbiased information and provide that information on the efficiency technologies that are available. What we've done is to develop these confidence reports which are objective assessments of the fuel efficiency savings of various technologies. We also work to build demand by actively engaging industry stakeholders. That includes industry leaders, early adopters, technology experts and original equipment manufacturers. We're constantly looking for new solutions to provide to our customers whether that's tied to vehicle specifications or fuel economy devices or new technologies that are being introduced into the marketplace. I'm certain that success in this means that the fleets and the manufacturers are sharing information with us that's not readily available in the marketplace already. It's probably the first time a collaborative effort like this has been done in the industry where you had OEMs that are competitors, manufacturers that are competitors, and a bunch of fleets that are competitors actually come together through trucking efficiency, sharing of that information. Success for us is taking that $70,000 in fuel expense and cutting it significantly. We've seen more change in the last 10 years than we've seen in the last 50. I think that's only going to accelerate in the years ahead and that's why we need efforts like this to really keep people looped to what's real and what's coming.
