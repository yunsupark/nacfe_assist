**Mike Roeth:** The trucks included vans and step vans, medium-duty box trucks, heavy-duty terminal tractors, and heavy-duty regional haul return-to-base operations. There are no sleeper tractors yet available, so there were no long-haul, disparate-route participants. We estimated the total power requirements of all trucks at the 10 depots at 214 megawatt-hours per day.

<!-- timestamp: 01:28 -->

> **Slide/Figure — Finding 1: Small Energy Depots**
> Type: video with text overlay
> Notes: Small energy depots are ready now.

**Mike Roeth:** Given all the experiences of the run, NACFE has some initial findings to share. First, is that small energy depots are ready now. Some sites have a lower number of trucks and/or run routes that are shorter and do not require much energy or big battery packs on the trucks. Urban trucks also tend to have long overnight dwell times, allowing for more cost-effective charging, improving their total cost of ownership. Finally, the amount of effort for the utility to provide this power is much lower.

<!-- timestamp: 02:00 -->

> **Slide/Figure — Finding 2: Large Energy Depots**
> Type: video with text overlays
> Notes:
> Large energy depots are gaining momentum.
> 410 miles on a single charge
> 1,076 miles in 24 hour day

**Mike Roeth:** Second, large energy depots are gaining momentum, given improvements in the trucks, faster charging, and improved efforts by utilities, construction companies, and others. One heavy-duty truck completed 410 miles on a single charge, and 1,076 miles in a 24-hour day hauling heavy beverages. Longer regional haul return-to-base operations thought not possible are becoming more practical, and we suggest fleets and their supporting organizations should all reevaluate their plans in this sector.

<!-- timestamp: 02:33 -->

> **Slide/Figure — Finding 3: Site Energization Timelines**
> Type: video with text overlays
> Notes:
> Energizing the sites takes too long.
> 9-36 months to get energized.

**Mike Roeth:** Lastly, energizing the sites to charge these trucks takes too long, without much confidence that this will improve. The 10 depots experienced 9 to up to 36 months to get energized, and this forced the fleets to get creative, deploying portable or temporary charging. Many actions need to take place to better match the availability of the trucks with the need to charge them on site.
