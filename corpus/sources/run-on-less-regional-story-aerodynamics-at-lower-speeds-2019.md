<!-- timestamp: 00:34 -->
**Dave Schaller:** For a lot of people when they think about regional haul, they aren't necessarily thinking of aerodynamics.
And I like to challenge them with this thought.
If you were out rowing a boat, it's a little easier for most people to relate to the resistance you get from water than it is to the resistance you get from wind.
And most people would never think of trying to row a rowboat backwards with a flat side into the water.
You naturally want to go with the more well-rounded front end of the boat into the direction of travel than you would the flat side.

<!-- timestamp: 01:05 -->
**Dave Schaller:** Well, think of a tractor-trailer that has no aerodynamics on it as that rowboat going backwards.
You've got the front of a dry van or reefer trailer into the wind, you're losing around 10% in your fuel economy just because of that trailer.
The most efficient boat you could want to be in as a personal rowing vessel would be a kayak or a canoe.
Think about trucks as an upside-down boat.
You want to look like a kayak or a canoe.
You want to present a smooth rounded front end and you want to ideally close the wake at the back of the trailer.
So those are the types of aerodynamic efficiencies that can make regional haul much more efficient as we move forward.
