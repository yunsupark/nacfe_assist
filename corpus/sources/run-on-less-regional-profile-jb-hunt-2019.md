**Tommy Cottingham:** To allow some flexibility depending on what our customers' needs are, if they grow in number of more stores and those stores are farther out, and we need to go a little bit farther distance and stay the night, then we're able to do that with having sleepers in the fleet. Our standard trailer tire is a low rolling resistance tire. Better fuel economy, as well as it helps us with our SmartWay certification for our trailers. Trailer side skirts, this is a composite skirt, so that helps us with weight and it's also a little bit more flexible. We have a thermoplastic piece attached to the bottom. We believe that having this additional, say, 10 or 11 inches at the bottom that flexes, that helps us with the railroad tracks and the curves and the stops. So another thing we've done at J.B. Hunt that's standard across all of our fleet is we've shortened up the CA, or cab to axle. What that has allowed us to do is shorten up our trailer gap. We get about a percent gain in MPGs. We also do run the full side skirts including the lower valance, as you see down here, you can see we're just a few inches off the ground. The shape of the hood is very aerodynamic. We do spec the Cummins X15 Efficiency Series, so we do spec the SmartAdvantage 400 horsepower spec engine. This is also done for fuel economy.
