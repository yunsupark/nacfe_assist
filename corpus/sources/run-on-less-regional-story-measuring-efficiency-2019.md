**Mike Roeth:** Measuring efficiency in regional haul is a pretty big challenge. There's a lot of different duty cycles and drive cycles with these trucks. Some are hauling very heavy. They go out at a full 80,000 pounds and have a diminishing load over the day, where others go out at maybe 20,000 pounds payload and maybe 65,000 GVW, and and go all the way out, drop that trailer, pick up another one, and come back. So, there's a lot of differences in in how they operate these trucks. So, sharing their efficiency is a pretty big challenge.

**Mike Roeth:** For Run On Less Regional, we've partnered with the Oak Ridge National Lab in Tennessee and the National Renewable Energy Lab in Colorado for a lot of analysis and understanding of how the trucks are performing and the drivers are driving within these particular 10 duty cycles. So, NREL out of Colorado is assisting us in instrumenting each of these trucks with a data logger. That data logger will be sending us a vast amount of data, and we'll be utilizing that for a number of things. One, we'll be using it to estimate payload. So, unlike over-the-road trucks where, you know, the the carrier or the truck picks up a load, maybe drive a day or two, and then unloads with the exact same freight, in regional haul we see multiple drops throughout the day. That's a very different duty cycle, an diminishing load duty cycle, and we'd like to understand exactly how much freight is being taken off at each of those drops to look at the fuel economy during the route as well as other things to best characterize that that particular cycle.

One, because of the vast difference in the duty cycles, it's pretty impossible to compare MPG of one truck versus another.
